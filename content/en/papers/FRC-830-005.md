## 0. Result in one paragraph

Present FRC reciprocity is not quantum Born reciprocity or a Majid-style
self-duality. The conclusion is structural, not rhetorical. The canonical
Born exchange and the FRC ledger involution have different composition and
orientation behavior. More decisively, the ledger datum
$(M_+,\omega,D)$ does not determine an action on the states that generate its
coordinates: inequivalent state involutions with different fixed sets can
induce the same $D$. One may place a standard primitive Hopf algebra on the
ambient ledger coordinate ring, where pullback by $D$ is a Hopf
automorphism, but that construction is inherited from the additive coordinate
plane and supplies neither a dual physical object nor a Hopf pairing,
semidualisation, representation exchange, or observable-state operation. In
every $C^1$ one-parameter arena with strictly monotone coherence and
$ds/dy < 1$ (including the decreasing-entropy trade $ds/dy < 0$ of FRC
830.004), $D$ has no nonidentity lift, and the threshold $ds/dy = 1$ is
sharp. The full qubit ledger is the first arena where the question is
dimensionally nontrivial, yet incoherent operations, unital channels, and
Wigner symmetries each fail on their stated domains, and an exact open-set
rigidity result excludes every affine map of the Bloch ball—hence every CPTP
qubit channel (unital or nonunital), every positive trace-preserving map, and
every fixed-ancilla Stinespring protocol—from inducing $D$ on any nonempty
open subset with nonzero off-diagonal coherence. Explicit one-dimensional
circle realizations show that the open-set hypothesis cannot be dropped. FRC
therefore retains an exact P2 ledger symmetry, typed P3 lift criteria, and
sharp obstruction theorems; promotion to Born or Majid self-duality is
rejected for the present evidence base.

## 2. The structures being compared

The word *reciprocity* occurs in several legitimate but inequivalent
mathematical settings. A comparison must begin by naming their objects and
maps.

### 2.1 The current FRC ledger datum

The canonical $k^*$ is the scale-invariant Boltzmann bridge. Its numerical
representation $k^*_{\mu}$ is determined by the units of the declared
information register $\mu$. For the natural-information representation used
in this paper, take $\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}$ and $k^*_{\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}}=1$, and define

$$
x = \frac{S_{\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}}}{k^*_{\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}}}, \qquad
y = \ln C_{\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}}, \qquad
M_+ = [0,\infty) \times (-\infty,0]. \tag{2}
$$

A lawful entropy-unit conversion multiplies $S_\mu$ and $k^*_\mu$ by the
same predeclared positive factor and leaves $C_\mu$ unchanged. It therefore
leaves $(x,y)$ unchanged. The comparison below concerns the normalized ledger
datum, not an independently evolving bridge or a dynamics on scale space.

The current exact ledger datum is

$$
\mathcal L = (M_+,\omega,D), \qquad
\omega = dx + dy, \qquad
D(x,y) = (-y,-x). \tag{3}
$$

Within the affine, invertible, kernel-preserving automorphisms of $M_+$,
FRC 830.001 proves

$$
D^2 = I, \qquad D^*\omega = -\omega, \tag{4}
$$

and proves that $D$ is the unique nonidentity involution in that stated class.
The entries $x$ and $y$ are derived scalar channels. Equation (3) by itself
contains no state space, observable algebra, coproduct, duality pairing,
representation category, Hamiltonian, symplectic form, or environment.

### 2.2 Canonical Born reciprocity

Born's reciprocity program exchanges position and momentum roles. After a
fixed conversion of units, its elementary linear exchange may be written
schematically as

$$
B_a(q,p) = \left(ap, -\frac{q}{a}\right), \qquad a \ne 0. \tag{5}
$$

The sign and scale make this a canonical phase-space exchange rather than a
mere swap of two scalars. With the canonical two-form

$$
\sigma = dq \wedge dp, \tag{6}
$$

one has $B_a^*\sigma = \sigma$. Born's wider program concerns spacetime and
momentum-energy variables, invariant phase-space structures, and elementary
particle theory. The historical proposal does not identify entropy with
negative log coherence.

### 2.3 Hopf duality and the Majid comparison gate

Majid's duality principle develops an observable-state or
position-momentum symmetry using Hopf algebras, quantum geometry,
representations, and quantum Fourier transformation. At the algebraic core,
a duality between Hopf algebras $H$ and $K$ may be expressed by a compatible
pairing

$$
\langle\ ,\ \rangle : H \times K \longrightarrow \mathbb C \tag{7}
$$

obeying, schematically,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle ab, c \rangle
&= \langle a \otimes b, \Delta_K c \rangle, \\
\langle a, cd \rangle
&= \langle \Delta_H a, c \otimes d \rangle, \\
\langle 1_H, c \rangle &= \epsilon_K(c), \qquad
\langle a, 1_K \rangle = \epsilon_H(a),
\end{aligned} \tag{8}
$$

with the corresponding antipode compatibility when the structures require
it. Topological, braided, and infinite-dimensional settings require suitable
refinements; equations (7)–(8) state only the algebraic comparison needed
here.

For this paper, a **Majid-comparison datum** records the minimum fields that a
positive FRC claim would have to supply:

$$
\mathfrak M = (H, K, \langle\ ,\ \rangle, \Sigma, \mathsf{Rep}, \mathcal I, \mathcal O). \tag{9}
$$

Here $H, K$ are independently declared algebraic or geometric objects,
$\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ is a compatible duality pairing, $\Sigma$ is an exchange
or semidualisation operation with a declared composition/equivalence rule,
$\mathsf{Rep}$ records representation-level meaning, $\mathcal I$ records the
preserved or exchanged structure, and $\mathcal O$ is the operational or
physical interpretation. This is a comparison checklist distilled from the
primary constructions, not a claim that every form of duality must use one
identical tuple.

### 2.4 Semidualisation

In Majid and Schroers' three-dimensional quantum-gravity examples,
semidualisation is an algebraic operation on quantum-group or matched-pair
data. It exchanges position and momentum roles, relates quantum doubles and
bicrossproduct-type models, acts on the associated representation theory, and
can interchange physical scales. The statement that a model is self-dual is
made at a specified algebraic level.

An involution of two derived real numbers does not supply that operation.
FRC would need to identify the matched or dually paired structures and show
that the operational entropy and coherence channels arise from them before
the word *semidualisation* could be used as a result.

## 3. Exact comparison with the canonical Born exchange

The superficial resemblance between (3) and (5) disappears once composition
and the canonical two-form are checked.

### Proposition 1. The FRC involution is not the canonical Born exchange

For every $a \ne 0$, the canonical Born map $B_a$ and the FRC ledger map $D$
obey

$$
B_a^2 = -I, \qquad B_a^4 = I, \qquad D^2 = I, \tag{10}
$$

and

$$
B_a^*(dq \wedge dp) = dq \wedge dp, \qquad D^*(dx \wedge dy) = -dx \wedge dy. \tag{11}
$$

Their matrices have determinants

$$
\det B_a = +1, \qquad \det D = -1. \tag{12}
$$

Consequently $B_a$ and $D$ are not conjugate by any bijection, and therefore
not by any real invertible linear, affine, smooth, or nonlinear change of
coordinates.

**Proof.** Direct substitution into (5) gives

$$
B_a^2(q,p) = B_a\!\left(ap, -\frac{q}{a}\right) = (-q, -p),
$$

and hence $B_a^4 = I$. Equation (4) gives $D^2 = I$. Differentiating (5),

$$
dq' = a\,dp, \qquad dp' = -\frac{1}{a}\,dq,
$$

so

$$
dq' \wedge dp' = a\,dp \wedge \left(-\frac{1}{a}\,dq\right) = dq \wedge dp.
$$

For $D$, $dx' = -dy$ and $dy' = -dx$, hence

$$
dx' \wedge dy' = dy \wedge dx = -dx \wedge dy.
$$

The determinant statements follow from the matrices

$$
[B_a] = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & a \\ -1/a & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
[D] = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & -1 \\ -1 & 0 \end{pmatrix}. \tag{13}
$$

The order already proves the strongest statement: conjugacy by a bijection
$h$ would give $(h B_a h^{-1})^2 = h B_a^2 h^{-1} \ne I$, whereas $D^2 = I$.
The determinant calculation independently excludes linear conjugacy and
records the different orientation behavior. $\square$

Proposition 1 does not say that every version of Born reciprocity must be the
single map (5). It proves the bounded comparison relevant to the visual
analogy usually made with the elementary position-momentum exchange. FRC's
$D$ has a different composition law and orientation behavior even before the
absence of physical phase-space variables is considered.

The sign $\det D = -1$ is not evidence that $D$ is antiunitary. Antiunitarity
is a statement about conjugate-linear action on a complex Hilbert space and
transition probabilities, not about the determinant of a two-coordinate real
ledger map. The actual Wigner-class obstruction is stated separately in
Section 6.

The form $dx \wedge dy$ in (11) is used only as the standard ambient area form
on the ledger plane. FRC has not independently established it as a physical
symplectic form. Equation (11) says that, if one makes this purely geometric
comparison, $D$ reverses that form while the canonical Born exchange preserves
its phase-space form.

## 4. The ledger does not reconstruct its underlying states

The decisive obstruction is not the sign in Proposition 1. It is the loss of
information in passing from independently structured states to two ledger
coordinates.

### Theorem 1. State-lift non-reconstruction

The ledger datum $\mathcal L = (M_+,\omega,D)$ does not determine a unique
state-space involution inducing $D$. In particular, there exist two
inequivalent involutive lifts on the same state space with different
fixed-state behavior and exactly the same ledger action.

**Proof.** First consider the identity presentation

$$
X_1 = M_+, \qquad \ell_1 = \operatorname{id}_{M_+}, \qquad \widetilde D_1 = D. \tag{14}
$$

It satisfies $\ell_1 \circ \widetilde D_1 = D \circ \ell_1$.

Now add a two-point fiber invisible to the ledger:

$$
X_2 = M_+ \times \{0,1\}, \qquad \ell_2(z,i) = z. \tag{15}
$$

Define

$$
\widetilde D_{2,0}(z,i) = (Dz, i), \qquad \widetilde D_{2,1}(z,i) = (Dz, 1-i). \tag{16}
$$

Because $D^2 = I$, both maps are involutions. Both make the same square
commute:

$$
\ell_2 \circ \widetilde D_{2,j} = D \circ \ell_2, \qquad j \in \{0,1\}. \tag{17}
$$

Their fixed-state behavior differs. If $z \in \operatorname{Fix}(D)$, then
every $(z,i)$ is fixed by $\widetilde D_{2,0}$. No $(z,i)$ is fixed by
$\widetilde D_{2,1}$, because $i = 1 - i$ has no solution in $\{0,1\}$. Thus
the two lifts cannot be conjugate by a bijection: conjugacy preserves the
fixed set. Nevertheless their ledger images and induced coordinate action are
identical. $\square$

### Corollary 1. The forgetful ledger map is non-injective

Forgetting the state-space fibers and retaining only $(M_+, D)$ loses enough
information to identify inequivalent structural actions. It therefore cannot
reconstruct, without extra input, a canonical state map, observable algebra,
duality pairing, or representation theory.

This is a non-reconstruction result, not a non-existence result. An
independently defined experiment, algebra, dynamics, boundary, or universal
property could select one lift. The theorem says that the ledger relation does
not perform that selection.

The two-point construction is the minimal counterexample, not the only source
of ambiguity. FRC 830.004, Theorem 4, gives a natural qubit-fiber version:
the full qubit state space has continuous fibers over the same entropy and
off-diagonal-coherence ledger point, and two inequivalent involutions in a
declared fiber trivialization induce the same $D$. The non-reconstruction is
therefore structural in an operational register actually used by FRC, rather
than an artifact of appending an invisible bit. Those lifts are set-theoretic
witnesses; they do not by themselves establish a physical quantum channel.

## 5. Why a function-algebra action is still insufficient

Every map of a space acts contravariantly on scalar functions. That fact is
useful, but it must not be mistaken for observable-state duality.

### Proposition 2. Minimal Hopf algebraization does not close the Majid gate

Let

$$
A = \mathbb R[x,y] \tag{18}
$$

be the polynomial coordinate algebra of the ambient ledger plane, with the
standard primitive Hopf structure

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta x &= x \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes x, &
\Delta y &= y \otimes 1 + 1 \otimes y, \\
\epsilon(x) &= \epsilon(y) = 0, &
\mathcal S(x) &= -x, \quad \mathcal S(y) = -y.
\end{aligned} \tag{19}
$$

Pullback along $D$ defines

$$
D^\sharp : A \longrightarrow A, \qquad
D^\sharp f = f \circ D, \qquad
D^\sharp x = -y, \qquad
D^\sharp y = -x. \tag{20}
$$

Then $D^\sharp$ is an involutive Hopf-algebra automorphism of $A$. This fact
does not define a Majid-style self-duality of an FRC operational register.

**Proof.** Pullback is multiplicative and unital:

$$
D^\sharp(fg) = (D^\sharp f)(D^\sharp g), \qquad D^\sharp 1 = 1. \tag{21}
$$

Since $D^2 = I$, $(D^\sharp)^2 = I$. On the generator $x$,

$$
\Delta(D^\sharp x) = \Delta(-y) = -y \otimes 1 - 1 \otimes y = (D^\sharp \otimes D^\sharp)\Delta x, \tag{22}
$$

and the same calculation holds for $y$. Unit, counit, and antipode
compatibility follow on the generators and therefore on all of $A$. $\square$

Proposition 2 records the strongest elementary algebraization available
without adding new physics. It also shows why saying merely "there is a Hopf
algebra" is not enough. The primitive Hopf structure in (19) comes from the
additive ambient coordinate plane; it would exist for any two linear
coordinates and is not derived from entropy, coherence, a bath, a quantum
state space, or an experimental operation. Moreover:

- $D^\sharp$ maps one chosen coordinate algebra to itself rather than pairing
  two independently defined observable and state structures;
- no nondegenerate pairing (7) is supplied between operational FRC objects;
- no matched-pair or bicrossproduct data is identified;
- no quantum Fourier transform or semidual model is constructed;
- no representation is exchanged with a geometry; and
- the antipode of the ambient additive group corresponds to $(x,y) \mapsto (-x,-y)$, which does not preserve the operational cone $M_+$ (for instance, $(1,-1) \in M_+$ maps to $(-1,1) \notin M_+$).

The algebraization is valid mathematics. Its evidential level is P2 ledger
geometry with an ambient algebra action, not P3/P4 structural or physical
self-duality.

## 6. General, operation-class, and open-set qubit lift boundaries

The non-reconstruction theorem says the ledger cannot choose a state action.
FRC 830.004 asks the stronger existence question first for a general
one-parameter trade and then for the full qubit state space. Section 6.2 then
resolves the open-set affine and CPTP question on the Bloch ball and proves
that its open-set hypothesis is sharp.

### Theorem 2. General one-parameter identity-lift obstruction

Let $t \mapsto (s(t), C(t))$ be a $C^1$ family on an interval $J$, with
$C(t) \in (0,1]$ strictly monotone and $C'(t) \ne 0$ on $J$ (or, more
generally, parameterized directly by $y = \ln C$ with $s(y)$ continuously
differentiable). Put $y = \ln C$ and suppose $ds/dy < 1$ on the parameter
domain—in particular, this holds in the decreasing-entropy trade regime
$ds/dy < 0$ of FRC 830.004. If $\tau$ induces $D$ on any subset of that
family, then

$$
\tau(t) = t, \qquad s(t) + y(t) = 0. \tag{23}
$$

Thus every lift is the identity restricted to the zero set of $u = s + y$,
and no nonidentity lift exists.

**Proof.** The coordinate $y$ parametrizes the family and

$$
\frac{dv}{dy} = \frac{ds}{dy} - 1 < 0, \qquad v = s - y, \tag{24}
$$

with the stronger bound $dv/dy < -1$ in the subcase $ds/dy < 0$. Hence $v$ is
strictly decreasing and therefore injective. Because $D$ preserves $v$, a
commuting lift satisfies $v(\tau(t)) = v(t)$ and therefore $\tau(t) = t$. Its
$u$ component then requires $u = -u$, hence $u = 0$. $\square$

No discreteness conclusion follows from $C^1$ regularity alone. Excluding an
open-domain identity lift additionally requires that $u^{-1}(0)$ have empty
interior; analyticity or explicit shape information can establish that fact.
For an exact normalized reciprocity curve,

$$
dS_\mu + k^*_\mu\,d\ln C = 0 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad ds + dy = 0, \tag{25}
$$

so $u = c$ on each connected component. If $c \ne 0$, there is no same-family
lift; if $c = 0$, the unique induced lift on a ledger-injective family is the
identity. This conclusion has no case split based on a numerical bridge
representation.

**Remark 1 (Exact sharpness at the threshold $ds/dy = 1$).** The condition
$ds/dy < 0$ of FRC 830.004 is sufficient but is not itself a sharp boundary:
the proof of Theorem 2 requires only $dv/dy = ds/dy - 1 < 0$, which extends
to the strictly larger class $ds/dy < 1$ (including nonnegative slopes
$0 \le ds/dy < 1$). The true sharp threshold is $ds/dy = 1$. On the open
interval $y \in (-3/2, -1/2)$, consider the affine family

$$
s(y) = 2 + y, \qquad
C(y) = e^y, \qquad
y \in \left(-\frac{3}{2}, -\frac{1}{2}\right), \qquad
\tau(y) = -2 - y. \tag{26}
$$

Every point of this family lies strictly inside $M_+$: for
$-3/2 < y < -1/2$, one has $s(y) = 2 + y \in (1/2, 3/2) \subset (0, \infty)$,
$y < 0$, and $C(y) = e^y \in (e^{-3/2}, e^{-1/2}) \subset (0, 1)$ strictly
increasing with $C'(y) = e^y > 0$. Moreover, $ds/dy \equiv 1$ (so
$v(y) = s(y) - y \equiv 2$ is constant), $\tau$ maps $(-3/2, -1/2)$
bijectively onto itself as a nonidentity involution ($\tau(\tau(y)) = y$,
fixing only $y = -1$), and direct substitution gives
$(s(\tau(y)), \tau(y)) = (-y, -(2+y)) = (-y, -s(y)) = D(s(y), y)$
identically. A smooth non-constant-slope witness showing breakdown whenever
$ds/dy$ crosses $+1$ is given by $s(t) = 1 + (t + t^2)/2$,
$y(t) = -1 + (t - t^2)/2$ on $t \in (-1/2, 1/2)$, where $s(t) \ge 7/8 > 0$,
$y(t) < -1/2 < 0$, $C'(t) > 0$, $ds/dy = (1 + 2t)/(1 - 2t)$ crosses $+1$ at
$t = 0$, and the nonidentity involution $\tau(t) = -t$ lifts $D$ identically.

The von Mises theorem of FRC 830.003 is an exact corollary of Theorem 2 with
stronger root information. There $v$ is strictly decreasing and $u$ is
strictly unimodal with exactly two zeros ($\kappa_- \approx 0.3314630060$ and
$\kappa_+ \approx 16.5644135142$, bracketing the maximum at
$\kappa_* \approx 1.6082794717$, $u_* \approx 0.9274570754$, and lying below
the entropy zero $\kappa_0 \approx 17.5949792765$), so $D$ has no lift on a
nonempty open concentration interval and no lift moving a von Mises density.
Its valid Legendre duality between $\kappa$ and $r$ is not the FRC ledger
exchange.

### 6.1 The qubit dimension threshold and operation-class obstructions

For a qubit state $\rho = \frac{1}{2}(I + \mathbf{r}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})$
with Bloch vector $\mathbf{r} = (r_x, r_y, r_z) \in B^3 = \{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb R^3 : \|\mathbf{r}\|\le 1\}$,
Bloch radius $R = \|\mathbf{r}\|$, fixed-basis off-diagonal coherence
$C_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sqrt{r_x^2 + r_y^2}$, and von Neumann entropy
$s(\mathbf{r}) = h\!\left(\frac{1+R}{2}\right)$ in nats (where
$h(p) = -p\ln p - (1-p)\ln(1-p)$), FRC 830.004 proves the exact ledger image

$$
\mathcal R = \{(s,y) : 0 \le s < \ln 2,\; y \le \ln R(s)\}, \qquad
K = \mathcal R \cap D(\mathcal R), \tag{27}
$$

where $R(s) \in (0, 1]$ is the inverse of $R \mapsto h((1+R)/2)$. The region
$K$ is nonempty and two-dimensional and contains a one-dimensional fixed leaf
$u = 0$ terminating at $R_\dagger \approx 0.610973770564$,
$s_\dagger = -\ln R_\dagger \approx 0.492701249431$. This is the dimension
threshold at which injectivity of one preserved coordinate no longer collapses
a proposed lift to the identity. It creates room for a lift question; it does
not answer that question.

### Proposition 3. Scoped physical-lift obstructions on $K$

On the fixed-basis qubit ledger:

1. no incoherent operation realizes $D$ on all of $K$, because $D$ strictly
   increases the declared coherence on the nonempty region $u < 0$ (witness
   $(0.2, -0.3) \in K$, where $e^{-0.2} > e^{-0.3}$);
2. no unital qubit CPTP channel realizes $D$ on all of $K$, because $D$
   strictly decreases entropy on the nonempty region $u > 0$ (witness
   $(0.3, -0.2) \in K$, where $0.2 < 0.3$); and
3. no unitary or antiunitary Wigner symmetry realizes $D$ at any point off
   $u = 0$, because both preserve the spectrum and hence entropy.

The third result is the **Wigner-class obstruction**. It derives from an
actual unitary or conjugate-linear state action, not from the orientation of
the ledger plane. These three hypotheses define different operation classes
and cannot by themselves be combined into a no-CPTP theorem.

### 6.2 Open-set no-affine-channel obstruction and 1D-circle sharpness

A natural question left open by FRC 830.004 is whether a single nonunital,
coherence-generating CPTP qubit channel—or a fixed-ancilla Stinespring
dilation—can induce $D$ on some nonempty open subset of the Bloch ball. On
sets with nonempty interior, the answer is strictly negative.

### Proposition 4. No affine Bloch map induces $D$ on an open set of qubit states

Let $E$ be a nonempty open subset of the open Bloch ball
$\operatorname{int}(B^3) = \{\mathbf{r}\in\mathbb R^3 : \|\mathbf{r}\| < 1\}$
on which $C_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sqrt{r_x^2 + r_y^2} > 0$. There is
no affine map $\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = T\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{t}$ of the Bloch ball
satisfying $\ell(\Phi(\mathbf{r})) = D(\ell(\mathbf{r}))$ for all
$\mathbf{r} \in E$. In particular, no CPTP qubit channel (unital or
nonunital), no positive trace-preserving map, and no fixed-ancilla
Stinespring protocol $\rho \mapsto \operatorname{Tr}_A[U(\rho \otimes \sigma)U^\dagger]$
(with fixed ancilla state $\sigma$) induces $D$ on $E$.

**Proof.** For every $\mathbf{r} \in E$, the second (log-coherence) component
of $\ell(\Phi(\mathbf{r})) = D(\ell(\mathbf{r}))$ requires
$\ln C_{\mathrm{off}}(\Phi(\mathbf{r})) = -s(\mathbf{r})$. Exponentiating
and squaring yields

$$
q(\mathbf{r}) := (T\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{t})_x^2 + (T\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{t})_y^2
= g(\|\mathbf{r}\|), \qquad
g(R) := e^{-2h((1+R)/2)}
= \left(\frac{1+R}{2}\right)^{1+R} \left(\frac{1-R}{2}\right)^{1-R}, \tag{28}
$$

for all $\mathbf{r} \in E$. Notice that $q(\mathbf{r})$ is a real polynomial
of total degree at most $2$ in $(r_x, r_y, r_z)$.

1. *$q$ is rotation-invariant.* Because $E$ is open in $\operatorname{int}(B^3)$
   and avoids $\mathbf{0}$ (since $C_{\mathrm{off}} > 0$ on $E$), choose
   $\mathbf{r}_0 \in E$ and an open ball $B = B(\mathbf{r}_0, \delta) \subset E$
   with $0 < \|\mathbf{r}_0\| - \delta < \|\mathbf{r}_0\| + \delta < 1$. Put
   $B' = B(\mathbf{r}_0, \delta/2) \subset B$. For every rotation
   $O \in SO(3)$ sufficiently close to the identity $I$ (specifically,
   $\|O - I\|_{\mathrm{op}} < \delta / (2(\|\mathbf{r}_0\| + \delta/2))$), we
   have $OB' \subset B \subset E$. For every $\mathbf{r} \in B'$,
   $q(O\mathbf{r}) = g(\|O\mathbf{r}\|) = g(\|\mathbf{r}\|) = q(\mathbf{r})$.
   The polynomial $q \circ O - q$ vanishes on the nonempty open ball $B'$, so
   it vanishes identically on $\mathbb R^3$. The stabilizer
   $\{O \in SO(3) : q \circ O = q\}$ is therefore a closed subgroup of the
   connected group $SO(3)$ containing a neighborhood of the identity, hence
   equals all of $SO(3)$. Writing $q(\mathbf{r}) = a + \mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}^T M \mathbf{r}$
   with $M = M^T$, $SO(3)$-invariance forces $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{0}$ and
   $M = b I_3$, so $q(\mathbf{r}) = a + b\|\mathbf{r}\|^2$ for constants
   $a, b \in \mathbb R$.
2. *$g(R)$ is not quadratic.* By Step 1, $g(R) = a + bR^2$ holds on the
   nonempty open radius interval
   $(\|\mathbf{r}_0\| - \delta, \|\mathbf{r}_0\| + \delta) \subset (0,1)$.
   Because $g$ is real-analytic on $(0,1)$, the identity theorem extends
   $g(R) = a + bR^2$ to all of $(0,1)$. Letting $R \to 0^+$ and $R \to 1^-$
   (using $\lim_{t\to 0^+} t\ln t = 0$) determines $a$ and $b$, yielding an
   immediate contradiction at $R = 1/2$:

$$
g(0^+) = \frac{1}{4}, \qquad g(1^-) = 1
\;\Longrightarrow\; a = \frac{1}{4},\; b = \frac{3}{4},
\qquad
g\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{3\sqrt{3}}{16} \approx 0.32476 \ne \frac{7}{16} = 0.4375. \tag{29}
$$

Finally, every CPTP qubit channel, every positive trace-preserving linear map
on $M_2(\mathbb C)$, and every fixed-ancilla protocol
$\rho \mapsto \operatorname{Tr}_A[U(\rho \otimes \sigma)U^\dagger]$ acts
affinely on the Bloch ball, so all are excluded on $E$. $\square$

Notice that the proof of Proposition 4 uses only the coherence component of
the lift condition; the entropy component is not even needed. Proposition 4
supersedes parts 1 and 2 of Proposition 3 on subsets of the Bloch ball with
nonempty interior, while Proposition 3 remains the decisive obstruction on
lower-dimensional subsets where monotonicity of coherence or entropy is
violated.

**Remark 2 (1D-circle sharpness of the open-set hypothesis).** The nonempty
open-set hypothesis in Proposition 4 is sharp and cannot be dropped to
arbitrary one-dimensional circles or two-dimensional surfaces of empty
interior in $B^3$:

1. *Off-leaf 1D circle via an explicit nonunital, coherence-generating CPTP
   channel.* Fix any $(s_0, y_0) \in K$ with $u_0 = s_0 + y_0 \ne 0$, for
   example $(s_0, y_0) = (0.2, -0.3) \in K$, whose image is
   $(s_1, y_1) = D(s_0, y_0) = (0.3, -0.2) \in K$. Put $R_j = R(s_j)$,
   $C_j = e^{y_j} \le R_j$, and $z_j = \sqrt{R_j^2 - C_j^2}$ for $j \in \{0,1\}$
   (numerically $R_0 \approx 0.89899$, $C_0 = e^{-0.3} \approx 0.74082$,
   $z_0 \approx 0.50928$; $R_1 \approx 0.82219$, $C_1 = e^{-0.2} \approx 0.81873$,
   $z_1 \approx 0.07531$). Consider the one-dimensional circle of qubit states
   and the constant replacement channel preparing $\rho_1 = \frac{1}{2}(I + \mathbf{r}_1\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ with $\mathbf{r}_1 = (C_1, 0, z_1)$:

$$
\mathcal C_{s_0, y_0} = \big\{(C_0\cos\phi,\, C_0\sin\phi,\, z_0) : \phi \in [0, 2\pi)\big\} \subset B^3, \qquad
\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}(\rho) = \operatorname{Tr}(\rho)\,\rho_1. \tag{30}
$$

   In Bloch coordinates, $\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}(\mathbf{r}) = 0\cdot\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{r}_1$
   is affine; in operator form, writing the spectral decomposition
   $\rho_1 = \sum_{a=\pm} \lambda_a |v_a\rangle\langle v_a|$, the four Kraus
   operators $K_{a,b} = \sqrt{\lambda_a}\,|v_a\rangle\langle b|$ ($a\in\{+,-\}$,
   $b\in\{0,1\}$) satisfy $\sum_{a,b} K_{a,b}^\dagger K_{a,b} = I$, so
   $\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}$ is a genuine nonunital, coherence-generating CPTP
   qubit channel. At every point $\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal C_{s_0, y_0}$ (indeed
   on the full two-circle fiber $\ell^{-1}(s_0, y_0)$),
   $\ell(\Phi_{\mathrm{rep}}(\mathbf{r})) = (0.3, -0.2) = D(0.2, -0.3) = D(\ell(\mathbf{r}))$,
   strictly changing the ledger coordinates. Proposition 4's contradiction does
   not trigger because $\mathcal C_{s_0, y_0}$ has empty interior in $B^3$ and
   constant radius $R_0$, so $q(\mathbf{r}) \equiv C_1^2 = g(R_0)$ holds
   identically on $\mathcal C_{s_0, y_0}$ without forcing $q \equiv g$ on an
   open ball.
2. *Fixed-leaf 1D circle (and 2D surface) via a non-identity bijective unitary
   CPTP channel.* On the fixed-leaf circle $\mathcal C_{0.3, -0.3}$ (where
   $s_0 = 0.3$, $y_0 = -0.3$, $u_0 = 0$, $R_0 \approx 0.82219$,
   $C_0 = e^{-0.3} \approx 0.74082$, $z_0 \approx 0.35664$)—and in fact on the
   entire two-dimensional fixed-leaf hypersurface $\ell^{-1}(K \cap \{u = 0\})$,
   which spans the open radius interval $(R_\dagger, 1)$—any $Z$-rotation
   unitary channel $\Phi_\alpha(\rho) = e^{-i\alpha\sigma_z/2}\rho e^{i\alpha\sigma_z/2}$
   ($\alpha \notin 2\pi\mathbb Z$) is a non-constant, bijective CPTP channel
   satisfying $\ell(\Phi_\alpha(\mathbf{r})) = D(\ell(\mathbf{r}))$. Again, the
   hypersurface is defined by $r_x^2 + r_y^2 = g(\|\mathbf{r}\|)$ and has empty
   interior in $B^3$, so the open-ball polynomial-identity step of Proposition 4
   does not apply.

**What remains genuinely open on the qubit ledger after Proposition 4.** Three
classes lie outside Proposition 4 and remain open for future testing:

1. non-constant CPTP realizations on other empty-interior subsets (curves or
   surfaces in $B^3$ not lying on a single fiber or the fixed leaf);
2. nonlinear, projective outcome-conditioned instrument maps
   $\rho \mapsto M\rho M^\dagger / \operatorname{Tr}(M\rho M^\dagger)$, which
   act fractionally rather than affinely on Bloch vectors; and
3. alternative coherence measures or bases, higher-dimensional quantum
   registers ($d \ge 3$), and cross-register maps.

### Theorem 3. Present Majid-gate obstruction

The structures established in FRC 830.000–830.004, together with the open-set
qubit-channel obstruction of Section 6.2, do not form a Majid-comparison datum
of the type (9), and they do not establish Born reciprocity.

**Proof.** Proposition 1 excludes identification of $D$ with the elementary
canonical Born exchange: the composition, determinant, and two-form behavior
differ. Theorem 1 proves that the ledger datum does not determine a state-level
exchange. Proposition 2 shows that even an involutive Hopf automorphism of the
ambient coordinate algebra supplies neither an independently defined dual
object nor the compatible pairing, semidualisation, or representation data in
(9). Theorem 2 rejects every nonidentity one-parameter lift under its stated
monotonicity hypotheses. FRC 830.004 makes non-reconstruction natural in the
qubit register, Proposition 3 excludes three major operation classes on their
stated domains, and Proposition 4 excludes every affine Bloch map—including
every CPTP qubit channel (unital or nonunital) and fixed-ancilla Stinespring
protocol—on any nonempty open subset with $C_{\mathrm{off}} > 0$, while
Remark 2 establishes the exact empty-interior boundary. At least the dual
pairing, semidual operation, representation-level meaning, and demonstrated
open-domain physical lift gates are therefore absent or failed. The present
structures cannot be promoted to the claimed class. $\square$

The word *present* is essential. Theorem 3 is stable under new notation but
not under genuinely new structure. If a later paper independently constructs
the missing objects or a valid open-class lift, those objects must be tested
by the reopening gate in Section 9 rather than ruled out by definition.

## 8. Relation to prior work

### 8.1 Born

Born's 1949 papers propose reciprocity between spacetime and
momentum-energy variables in elementary-particle theory. Proposition 1 uses
only the elementary canonical sign exchange, not the full historical theory.
The comparison result is therefore narrow: the FRC reflection-like involution
is not the same linear transformation as the canonical phase-space rotation.
No claim is made that this observation evaluates Born's wider program.

### 8.2 Majid's duality principle

Majid's 1994 account explicitly frames the duality principle as a symmetry
between observables and states enabled by Hopf algebras and quantum geometry.
The dual Hopf structures, compatible pairings, and representation-theoretic
interpretation do the mathematical work. His later account describes quantum
Born reciprocity through Fourier duality and its non-Abelian/Hopf-algebraic
generalisations.

FRC presently agrees with the methodological demand—name the structures and
maps—but does not satisfy the construction. Its exact involution is on a
two-dimensional ledger image. Section 4 proves why that image cannot recover
the underlying action.

### 8.3 Majid and Schroers' semidualisation

The three-dimensional quantum-gravity analysis relates concrete quantum-group
models by an algebraic semidualisation, follows their limits and scale
exchanges, and studies their representations as wave equations. It also states
the algebraic level at which a $q$-deformed model is self-dual. No analogous
matched quantum-group data, representation family, or model exchange has been
derived from FRC entropy and coherence.

### 8.4 Information geometry is a genuine but different duality

The von Mises exponential family has natural coordinate $\kappa$, expectation
coordinate $r$, convex potential $\psi$, and Legendre dual $\psi^* = -S$. That
duality is mathematically established. Theorem 2 shows that the different map
$D(S, \ln r) = (-\ln r, -S)$ does not induce a nonidentity action on the same
family. Rejecting the FRC lift preserves rather than weakens the valid
information-geometric structure.

### 9.1 Kill result

The paper's kill condition is met. Shared vocabulary, scalar exchange,
$D^2 = I$, $D^*\omega = -\omega$, category syntax, or an automatically induced
function-algebra automorphism do not establish Born reciprocity or Majid-style
self-duality. The one-parameter theorem rejects nontrivial lifts under broad
monotonicity hypotheses, and the first two-dimensional operational arena
rejects not only incoherent operations, unital channels, and Wigner
symmetries, but every affine map and CPTP qubit channel on any nonempty open
subset of the Bloch ball with nonzero coherence. Present FRC is therefore
**not promoted** beyond ledger geometry, typed operational morphisms, and
sharp obstruction results.

This kill result does not erase the positive mathematics. FRC retains:

- the unique exact affine sign-reversing involution $D$ on $M_+$ in the stated
  class;
- the weak and strong register categories;
- an exact embedding of the von Mises family into the broader phase-density
  register;
- the von Mises family's genuine Legendre geometry;
- a general one-parameter identity-lift obstruction for $ds/dy < 1$ (with
  sharp threshold $ds/dy = 1$, specializing to $ds/dy < 0$) and exact qubit
  dimension threshold;
- natural qubit-fiber non-reconstruction; and
- scoped physical-lift obstructions together with the sharp open-set
  no-affine-channel theorem on the qubit Bloch ball.

### 9.2 Reopening gate

A future positive comparison must supply, before promotion:

1. independently defined algebraic, geometric, state, or observable objects;
2. the relevant product and coproduct or a comparably explicit structure;
3. a compatible and suitably nondegenerate duality pairing;
4. an exchange, Fourier, or semidualisation map not fitted from the desired
   ledger identity;
5. a composition/equivalence or self-duality rule;
6. a nontrivial state/model lift inducing the ledger action;
7. representation-level or dynamical consequences; and
8. an operational interpretation with units and boundaries declared.

FRC 830.004 and Proposition 4 sharpen the lift gate, but neither supplies a
Hopf pairing or semidualisation. A future projective-instrument, empty-interior,
or higher-dimensional construction would contribute only to the lift gate.
Each gate remains separate.

## 10. What this paper does not establish

This paper does not establish:

- that no future FRC algebra, coalgebra, pairing, or duality can exist;
- a classification of all nonlinear lifts, projective instrument maps, or
  cross-register maps;
- a no-go theorem for CPTP realizations restricted to empty-interior subsets
  (curves or surfaces) of the Bloch ball, or for higher-dimensional ($d \ge 3$)
  quantum channels;
- a new Hopf algebra, bicrossproduct, braided group, or quantum Fourier
  transform;
- a refutation of Born reciprocity or Majid's program;
- a position-momentum interpretation of entropy and coherence;
- a quantum-gravity model;
- a physical state-observable exchange;
- a running $k^*$ coupling, beta function, scale connection, or independent
  bridge dynamics;
- a universal law for $dS + k^*\,d\ln C = 0$; or
- empirical evidence for FRC self-duality.

The conclusion is narrower and exact: the structures currently established
by FRC do not meet the comparison gates, the ledger data alone cannot
reconstruct the missing action, and no affine or CPTP qubit channel can induce
$D$ on an open coherent subset of the Bloch ball.
