## 1. Position in the corpus, continuity, and scope split

In the 2025 first version of `FRC 100.006` (Zenodo DOI `10.5281/zenodo.17438360`), the Born rule was framed as a dynamical statistical equilibrium of microstates evolving in a coherence potential $\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \ln C$, analogous to the Boltzmann equilibrium of classical statistical mechanics. That early draft established the enduring conceptual direction of the `FRC 100.006` line—representing quantum outcome statistics as an equilibrium attractor rather than an isolated postulate—while overclaiming three points that subsequent audits exposed as unproved or circular:

1. it substituted $C(x) = |\psi(x)|^2$ and $\Lambda_0 = k^*$ directly into $\rho_{\mathrm{eq}} \propto \exp(\Lambda / k^*)$ and announced a first-principles derivation of the Born rule, hiding the circularity of inserting quadratic weights into $C$;
2. it simultaneously described the dynamics as deterministic and wrote a Fokker–Planck equation with nonzero diffusion $D$, without distinguishing deterministic basin capture ($D = 0$) from noisy ergodic equilibrium ($D > 0$); and
3. it quoted uncalibrated physical numbers ($\tau_{\mathrm{relax}} \sim 10^{-12}\text{ s}$ and resonant-driving deviations $|\delta(x)| \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$) without deriving them from an instrumented coupling constant or relaxation generator.

Version `v2.0` retains `FRC 100.006` firmly in its original Born-rule and resonant-equilibrium category while replacing every heuristic or circular step with an exact, machine-verified mathematical architecture aligned with the hardened frontier corpus (`FRC 100.003 v3.3`, `FRC 100.005 v2.3`, `FRC 100.007 v3.3`, `FRC 566.001 v2.2`, `FRC 787.787 v1.2`, and `FRC 830.005 v0.3`). The core methodological discipline is a clean **two-half split**:

> **Half A (Proven Conditional Core):** Given a multi-basin coherence landscape $C(\phi; \alpha, \beta)$ on the microstate phase circle $S^1$ weighted by $|\alpha_j|^2$ and driven by overdamped Fokker–Planck dynamics with Einstein-scaled diffusivity $D = k^*/\eta$, prove exactly when, how fast, and with what finite-$\beta$ error the stationary basin occupancies converge to the Born probabilities $|\alpha_j|^2$—and prove that $\gamma = \Lambda_0 / k^* = 1$ and $D > 0$ are both mathematically necessary.
>
> **Half B (Open Admissibility Gates):** State without evasion the four physical and structural obligations that Half A does not discharge: (i) the non-circular origin of $|\alpha_j|^2$ landscape weighting and $\Lambda_0 = k^*$; (ii) operational equivalence across distinct preparations of the same density matrix; (iii) bipartite no-signaling; and (iv) explicit environment-channel entropy accounting.

Companion paper `FRC 100.006.002 v2.0` develops the non-equilibrium half of this program—replacing the retired $[10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$ guess with exact spectral-gap, preparation-skew, and adiabatic-ramp diagnostic bounds derived directly from the Fokker–Planck generator established here.

## 2. Exact mathematical model on the phase circle $S^1$

Let the microstate phase coordinate reside on the unit circle $S^1 = \mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})$ equipped with Lebesgue measure $d\phi$ (so $\int_0^{2\pi} d\phi = 2\pi$) and geodesic angular metric $d_{S^1}(\phi, \psi) := \arccos(\cos(\phi - \psi)) \in [0, \pi]$. Let $\{|j\rangle\}_{j=1}^m$ ($m \ge 2$) denote a predeclared orthonormal pointer basis associated with $m$ distinct pointer angles $\phi_1, \dots, \phi_m \in S^1$, with minimum pairwise angular separation
$\Delta_{\min} := \min_{j \ne l} d_{S^1}(\phi_j, \phi_l) > 0$ and half-separation $\delta := \Delta_{\min}/2 \in (0, \pi/2]$.

Given a normalized state vector $|\psi\rangle = \sum_{j=1}^m \alpha_j |j\rangle$ with positive outcome weights $w_j := |\alpha_j|^2 > 0$ ($\sum_{j=1}^m w_j = 1$) and inverse angular sharpness (basin gain) $\beta > 0$, define the smooth multi-basin **coherence landscape** on $S^1$:

$$
C(\phi; \alpha, \beta) := \sum_{j=1}^m |\alpha_j|^2 \exp\!\big(\beta \cos(\phi - \phi_j)\big). \tag{1}
$$

Because $w_j > 0$ and $\sum_{j=1}^m w_j = 1$, the landscape satisfies $e^{-\beta} \le C(\phi; \alpha, \beta) \le e^{\beta}$ identically on $S^1$; in particular, $C \in C^\infty(S^1, (0, \infty))$ is strictly positive and smooth everywhere. Following the operational typing of `FRC 100.007 v3.3` and `FRC 787.787 v1.2`, define the **coherence potential**

$$
\Lambda(\phi) := \Lambda_0 \ln C(\phi; \alpha, \beta), \tag{2}
$$

where $\Lambda_0 > 0$ sets the potential scale. In this paper, $\Lambda(\phi)$ is an observation-derived equilibrium landscape $\Lambda_{\mathrm{eq}}$ on the microstate coordinate $S^1$, not an asserted fundamental spacetime field.

To keep all physical dimensions unambiguous, let $\eta > 0$ denote the overdamped angular friction coefficient (units: $[\text{potential}\cdot\text{time}/\text{rad}^2]$), let $k^* > 0$ denote the canonical entropy–coherence bridge constant in the declared register (units: $[\text{potential}]$, identical to $\Lambda_0$; in nats-normalized registers $k^* = 1$ as in `FRC 566.001 v2.2` and `FRC 100.003 v3.3` §9), and define the stochastic angular diffusivity $D > 0$ (units: $[\text{rad}^2/\text{time}]$) and dimensionless **exponent ratio** $\gamma > 0$ by the exact Einstein relation:

$$
D := \frac{k^*}{\eta}, \qquad \gamma := \frac{\Lambda_0}{k^*} = \frac{\Lambda_0}{\eta D} > 0. \tag{3}
$$

The overdamped Itô Langevin equation for a microstate phase $\phi_t \in S^1$ climbing $\Lambda(\phi)$ against stochastic angular diffusion $D$ is

$$
d\phi_t = \frac{1}{\eta}\,\partial_\phi \Lambda(\phi_t)\,dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW_t = D\gamma\,\partial_\phi \ln C(\phi_t; \alpha, \beta)\,dt + \sqrt{2D}\,dW_t, \tag{4}
$$

where $W_t$ is standard one-dimensional Brownian motion wrapped on $S^1$. Note that the numerical pilot of `FRC 100.003 v3.3` §9 is the exact special case $\eta = 1$, $k^* = 1$, $\Lambda_0 = 1$ ($D = 1$, $\gamma = 1$). The probability density $\rho(\phi, t)$ on $S^1$ (with $\rho \ge 0$ and $\int_0^{2\pi} \rho(\phi, t)\,d\phi = 1$) obeys the Fokker–Planck (Smoluchowski) conservation law

$$
\partial_t \rho(\phi, t) = -\partial_\phi J[\rho](\phi, t), \qquad J[\rho] := \frac{1}{\eta}(\partial_\phi \Lambda)\rho - D\,\partial_\phi \rho = D\left(\gamma\,\rho\,\partial_\phi \ln C - \partial_\phi \rho\right). \tag{5}
$$

## 3. Exact conditional equilibrium, H-theorem, and exponent uniqueness (Half A)

We now prove the three load-bearing theorems of Half A and the contrasting deterministic-capture obstruction.

### Theorem 1. Unique zero-current stationary density on $S^1$

For every $C \in C^\infty(S^1, (0,\infty))$, $\eta > 0$, $k^* > 0$, and $\Lambda_0 > 0$ (with $D = k^*/\eta$ and $\gamma = \Lambda_0/k^*$), the Fokker–Planck equation (5) admits a unique smooth stationary probability density $\rho^*_\gamma \in C^\infty(S^1, (0,\infty))$, given explicitly by

$$
\rho^*_\gamma(\phi) = \frac{1}{Z(\beta, \gamma)}\exp\!\left(\frac{\Lambda(\phi)}{k^*}\right) = \frac{1}{Z(\beta, \gamma)}\,C(\phi; \alpha, \beta)^\gamma, \qquad Z(\beta, \gamma) := \int_0^{2\pi} C(\phi; \alpha, \beta)^\gamma\,d\phi. \tag{6}
$$

Moreover, $\rho^*_\gamma$ satisfies exact detailed balance on $S^1$: $J[\rho^*_\gamma](\phi) \equiv 0$ for all $\phi \in S^1$.

**Proof.** Direct differentiation of Eq. (6) gives $\partial_\phi \rho^*_\gamma = \gamma\,\rho^*_\gamma\,\partial_\phi \ln C$, whence $J[\rho^*_\gamma] = D(\gamma\,\rho^*_\gamma\,\partial_\phi \ln C - \partial_\phi \rho^*_\gamma) \equiv 0$ and $\partial_t \rho^*_\gamma = -\partial_\phi J[\rho^*_\gamma] \equiv 0$. To prove uniqueness on the periodic circle $S^1$ without assuming vanishing current a priori, let $\rho$ be any smooth periodic stationary density. Then $\partial_\phi J[\rho] = 0$ implies $J[\rho](\phi) \equiv J_0$ for some constant $J_0 \in \mathbb{R}$. Dividing $J[\rho] = D(\gamma \rho\,\partial_\phi\ln C - \partial_\phi\rho) = J_0$ by $-D\,C(\phi)^\gamma < 0$ (since $D > 0$ and $C(\phi) > 0$) yields the exact integrating-factor identity

$$
\partial_\phi\!\left(\frac{\rho(\phi)}{C(\phi)^\gamma}\right) = -\frac{J_0}{D\,C(\phi)^\gamma}. \tag{7}
$$

Integrating Eq. (7) around $S^1$ from $0$ to $2\pi$ and using periodicity of both $\rho$ and $C$ gives $0 = -(J_0/D)\int_0^{2\pi} C(\phi)^{-\gamma}\,d\phi$. Because $C(\phi) > 0$ everywhere on compact $S^1$, the integral is strictly positive, forcing $J_0 = 0$. Hence $\rho(\phi)/C(\phi)^\gamma$ is constant on $S^1$, and normalization $\int_0^{2\pi}\rho(\phi)\,d\phi = 1$ uniquely forces $\rho \equiv \rho^*_\gamma$. $\square$

### Theorem 2. Exact relative-entropy H-theorem and exponential convergence

Let $\rho(\phi, t) > 0$ be any smooth positive probability density solving Eq. (5) on $S^1$, and define the Kullback–Leibler divergence (relative entropy) with respect to $\rho^*_\gamma$:

$$
H(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma) := \int_0^{2\pi} \rho(\phi, t)\ln\!\left(\frac{\rho(\phi, t)}{\rho^*_\gamma(\phi)}\right) d\phi \ge 0. \tag{8}
$$

Then:

1. $H(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma)$ satisfies the exact dissipation identity
$$
\frac{d}{dt} H(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma) = -D \int_0^{2\pi} \rho(\phi, t)\left|\partial_\phi \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho(\phi, t)}{\rho^*_\gamma(\phi)}\right)\right|^2 d\phi \le 0. \tag{9}
$$
2. Equality $dH/dt = 0$ holds if and only if $\rho(\cdot, t) \equiv \rho^*_\gamma$ identically on $S^1$.
3. Defining the potential oscillation $\operatorname{osc}(V) := \max_{\phi \in S^1} V(\phi) - \min_{\phi \in S^1} V(\phi)$ for $V(\phi) := \gamma\ln C(\phi)$, the stationary measure $\rho^*_\gamma$ satisfies a Log-Sobolev inequality with Holley–Stroock constant $\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}} \ge e^{-\operatorname{osc}(V)} \ge e^{-2\gamma\beta} > 0$, yielding explicit global exponential convergence in both relative entropy and total variation:
$$
H(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma) \le H(\rho(\cdot, 0) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma)\,e^{-2D\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}}\,t}, \qquad \frac{1}{2}\|\rho(\cdot, t) - \rho^*_\gamma\|_{L^1(S^1)} \le \sqrt{\frac{1}{2} H(\rho(\cdot, 0) \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma)}\,e^{-D\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}}\,t}. \tag{10}
$$

**Proof.** Rewrite the Fokker–Planck flux in Eq. (5) as $J[\rho] = -D\,\rho\,\partial_\phi \ln(\rho/\rho^*_\gamma)$, so $\partial_t \rho = D\,\partial_\phi\big(\rho\,\partial_\phi\ln(\rho/\rho^*_\gamma)\big)$. Differentiating Eq. (8), using $\int_0^{2\pi} \partial_t\rho\,d\phi = 0$, and integrating by parts on periodic $S^1$ (zero boundary terms) gives

$$
\frac{d}{dt} H(\rho \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma) = \int_0^{2\pi} (\partial_t\rho)\ln\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho^*_\gamma}\right) d\phi = -D \int_0^{2\pi} \rho\left|\partial_\phi \ln\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho^*_\gamma}\right)\right|^2 d\phi \le 0,
$$

establishing Eq. (9). Since $D > 0$ and $\rho(\phi, t) > 0$ on connected $S^1$, the integral vanishes if and only if $\partial_\phi\ln(\rho/\rho^*_\gamma) \equiv 0$, i.e., $\rho/\rho^*_\gamma$ is constant; normalization forces $\rho \equiv \rho^*_\gamma$. For Eq. (10), the uniform probability measure $\mu_0 = d\phi/(2\pi)$ on $S^1$ obeys the sharp Log-Sobolev inequality $H(\rho \,\|\, \mu_0) \le \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \rho |\partial_\phi\ln(\rho/\mu_0)|^2\,d\phi$ (Log-Sobolev constant $\rho_0 = 1$). Because $\rho^*_\gamma = Z^{-1} e^{V} (2\pi\mu_0)$ is a bounded perturbation of $\mu_0$, the Holley–Stroock perturbation theorem (1987) yields $\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}}(\rho^*_\gamma) \ge \rho_0 e^{-\operatorname{osc}(V)} = e^{-\operatorname{osc}(V)} \ge e^{-2\gamma\beta}$, where the last inequality follows from $e^{-\beta} \le C(\phi) \le e^{\beta}$. Substituting $I(\rho \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma) \ge 2\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}} H(\rho \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma)$ into Eq. (9) gives $dH/dt \le -2D\rho_{\mathrm{LSI}} H$; integrating via Gronwall's inequality and applying Pinsker's inequality $\frac{1}{2}\|\rho - \rho^*_\gamma\|_{L^1} \le \sqrt{H/2}$ establishes Eq. (10). $\square$

**Remark 1 (Actual spectral gap versus Holley–Stroock floor).** The Holley–Stroock constant $e^{-\operatorname{osc}(V)}$ in Eq. (10) provides an explicit, closed-form guarantee of global exponential convergence for every $(\beta, \gamma)$, but it is conservative at moderate $\beta$. In `FRC 100.006.002 v2.0`, the exact self-adjoint Fokker–Planck spectral gap $\lambda_1(\beta)$ is computed directly; for the canonical 3-basin benchmark at $\beta = 4, \gamma = 1, D = 1$, $\lambda_1 = 0.622821$ whereas the Holley–Stroock floor is $e^{-\operatorname{osc}(V)} = 0.06260$.

### Theorem 3. Exact $\gamma = \Lambda_0/k^*$ exponent limit and non-asymptotic finite-$\beta$ sandwich bound

Partition $S^1$ into the open Voronoi pointer basins $B_1, \dots, B_m$ around the distinct pointer angles $\phi_1, \dots, \phi_m$:

$$
B_j := \left\{\phi \in S^1 : d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_j) < d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_l)\ \text{for all } l \ne j\right\}, \qquad p_j(\beta, \gamma) := \int_{B_j} \rho^*_\gamma(\phi)\,d\phi = \frac{I_j(\beta, \gamma)}{\sum_{l=1}^m I_l(\beta, \gamma)}, \tag{11}
$$

where $I_j(\beta, \gamma) := \int_{B_j} C(\phi; \alpha, \beta)^\gamma\,d\phi$ (the boundary $\bigcup_j \partial B_j$ has at most $m$ points and Lebesgue measure zero). Let $\delta := \Delta_{\min}/2 \in (0, \pi/2]$, and fix any inner core radius $\theta_0 \in (0, \delta)$. Then for every $j \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, every $\beta > 0$, and every $\gamma > 0$, the rescaled basin integral satisfies the exact non-asymptotic two-sided bound

$$
w_j^\gamma \Big(1 - \epsilon_{\mathrm{tail}}(\gamma\beta, \delta)\Big) \le \frac{I_j(\beta, \gamma)}{2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta)} \le w_j^\gamma \big(1 + \eta_j(\beta, \theta_0)\big)^\gamma + \epsilon_{\mathrm{shell}}(\gamma\beta, \theta_0), \tag{12}
$$

where $I_0(z) = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^\pi e^{z\cos\theta}\,d\theta$ is the modified Bessel function of the first kind and the three explicit, exponentially decaying error terms are

$$
\epsilon_{\mathrm{tail}}(z, \delta) := \frac{(\pi - \delta)\,e^{z\cos\delta}}{\pi I_0(z)}, \qquad \eta_j(\beta, \theta_0) := \frac{1 - w_j}{w_j}\,e^{-\beta\Delta_c(\theta_0)}, \qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{shell}}(z, \theta_0) := \frac{e^{z\cos\theta_0}}{I_0(z)}, \tag{13}
$$

with strictly positive geometric gap $\Delta_c(\theta_0) := \cos\theta_0 - \cos(2\delta - \theta_0) = 2\sin(\delta)\sin(\delta - \theta_0) > 0$. Writing $L_j(\beta, \gamma)$ and $U_j(\beta, \gamma)$ for the left- and right-hand sides of Eq. (12), whenever $L_j > 0$ for all $j$ the equilibrium basin occupancy satisfies the certified finite-$\beta$ enclosure

$$
p_j^{\min}(\beta, \gamma) := \frac{L_j}{L_j + \sum_{l \ne j} U_l} \le p_j(\beta, \gamma) \le \frac{U_j}{U_j + \sum_{l \ne j} L_l} =: p_j^{\max}(\beta, \gamma), \tag{14}
$$

and in the sharp-basin limit $\beta \to \infty$,

$$
\lim_{\beta \to \infty} p_j(\beta, \gamma) = p_j^*(\gamma) := \frac{|\alpha_j|^{2\gamma}}{\sum_{l=1}^m |\alpha_l|^{2\gamma}} \qquad (j = 1, \dots, m). \tag{15}
$$

**Proof.** For the lower bound in Eq. (12), every summand in Eq. (1) is positive, so $C(\phi) \ge w_j e^{\beta\cos(\phi-\phi_j)}$ on $B_j$. Because $B_j \supseteq (\phi_j - \delta, \phi_j + \delta)$, we obtain

$$
I_j(\beta, \gamma) \ge w_j^\gamma \int_{-\delta}^{\delta} e^{\gamma\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta = w_j^\gamma \left(2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta) - \int_{|\theta| \ge \delta} e^{\gamma\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta\right) \ge w_j^\gamma\left(2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta) - 2(\pi - \delta)e^{\gamma\beta\cos\delta}\right),
$$

since $\cos\theta \le \cos\delta$ for $|\theta| \in [\delta, \pi]$. Dividing by $2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta)$ proves the left inequality of Eq. (12).

For the upper bound, split $B_j = U_j \cup (B_j \setminus U_j)$, where $U_j := \{\phi \in S^1 : d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_j) \le \theta_0\} \subset B_j$. On $U_j$, the triangle inequality gives $d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_l) \ge \Delta_{\min} - \theta_0 = 2\delta - \theta_0$ for all $l \ne j$, hence $\cos(\phi - \phi_j) \ge \cos\theta_0$ and $\cos(\phi - \phi_l) \le \cos(2\delta - \theta_0)$. Thus on $U_j$,

$$
\frac{C(\phi)}{w_j e^{\beta\cos(\phi-\phi_j)}} = 1 + \sum_{l \ne j} \frac{w_l}{w_j} e^{\beta(\cos(\phi-\phi_l) - \cos(\phi-\phi_j))} \le 1 + \frac{1 - w_j}{w_j} e^{-\beta\Delta_c(\theta_0)} = 1 + \eta_j(\beta, \theta_0),
$$

so $\int_{U_j} C(\phi)^\gamma\,d\phi \le w_j^\gamma (1 + \eta_j(\beta, \theta_0))^\gamma \int_{-\theta_0}^{\theta_0} e^{\gamma\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta \le w_j^\gamma (1 + \eta_j(\beta, \theta_0))^\gamma \cdot 2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta)$. On the outer shell $B_j \setminus U_j$, the Voronoi definition of $B_j$ implies $d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_l) \ge d_{S^1}(\phi, \phi_j) > \theta_0$ for **every** $l \in \{1, \dots, m\}$, whence $\cos(\phi - \phi_l) \le \cos\theta_0$ for all $l$ and therefore $C(\phi) \le (\sum_{l=1}^m w_l) e^{\beta\cos\theta_0} = e^{\beta\cos\theta_0}$. Integrating over $B_j \setminus U_j$ (whose measure is strictly less than $2\pi$) gives $\int_{B_j \setminus U_j} C(\phi)^\gamma\,d\phi \le 2\pi e^{\gamma\beta\cos\theta_0}$. Adding both regions and dividing by $2\pi I_0(\gamma\beta)$ proves the right inequality of Eq. (12).

Eq. (14) follows immediately from monotonicity of $x / (x + y)$ in $x > 0$ and $y > 0$. Finally, because $I_0(z) \sim e^z/\sqrt{2\pi z}$ as $z \to \infty$ while $\cos\delta < 1$, $\cos\theta_0 < 1$, and $\Delta_c(\theta_0) > 0$, all three error terms in Eq. (13) vanish exponentially as $\beta \to \infty$. Therefore $L_j(\beta, \gamma) \to w_j^\gamma$ and $U_j(\beta, \gamma) \to w_j^\gamma$, and Eq. (14) yields Eq. (15). $\square$

### Corollary 1. Uniqueness of $\gamma = \Lambda_0/k^* = 1$ and power-law distortion sensitivity

Suppose the state $|\psi\rangle$ has at least two outcomes $j, k$ with unequal nonzero weights $|\alpha_j|^2 \ne |\alpha_k|^2$. Then in the sharp-basin limit $\beta \to \infty$,

$$
\frac{p_j^*(\gamma)}{p_k^*(\gamma)} = \left(\frac{|\alpha_j|^2}{|\alpha_k|^2}\right)^{\!\gamma}, \qquad p_j^*(\gamma) = |\alpha_j|^2\ \forall j \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \gamma = \frac{\Lambda_0}{k^*} = 1 \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \Lambda_0 = k^*. \tag{16}
$$

Furthermore, let $F(\gamma) := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^m |p_j^*(\gamma) - w_j|$ denote the total-variation distance from Born weights and $H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w) := -\sum_{l=1}^m w_l \ln w_l$ the Shannon entropy of $w$. Around $\gamma = 1$, each coordinate $p_j^*(\gamma)$ is smooth with nonzero derivative vector, while $F(\gamma)$ has a sharp V-shaped minimum (cusp) with exact one-sided derivatives $\partial_\pm F(1) = \pm A(w)$:

$$
\left.\frac{\partial p_j^*(\gamma)}{\partial \gamma}\right|_{\gamma = 1} = w_j\big(\ln w_j + H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w)\big), \qquad \partial_\pm F(1) = \pm A(w), \qquad F(\gamma) = A(w)\,|\gamma - 1| + O\!\left((\gamma - 1)^2\right), \tag{17}
$$

where $A(w) := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^m w_j\big|\ln w_j + H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w)\big| > 0$.

**Proof.** Eq. (16) follows directly from Eq. (15): since $w_j / w_k \ne 1$, $(w_j/w_k)^\gamma = w_j/w_k$ has the unique real solution $\gamma = 1$. Differentiating $p_j^*(\gamma) = w_j^\gamma / \sum_l w_l^\gamma$ at $\gamma = 1$ with $\sum_l w_l = 1$ gives $d_j := \partial_\gamma p_j^*|_1 = w_j\ln w_j - w_j\sum_l w_l\ln w_l = w_j(\ln w_j + H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w))$, whence $p_j^*(\gamma) - w_j = d_j(\gamma - 1) + O((\gamma - 1)^2)$. Taking absolute values and summing over $j$ yields $F(\gamma) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_j |d_j|\,|\gamma - 1| + O((\gamma - 1)^2) = A(w)\,|\gamma - 1| + O((\gamma - 1)^2)$. Because $\ln w_j + H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w)$ has expectation zero under $w$ and is non-constant whenever $w$ is non-uniform, at least one $d_j$ is strictly nonzero, so $A(w) > 0$. Consequently the ordinary two-sided derivative $F'(1)$ does not exist, whereas the exact right and left derivatives at $\gamma = 1$ are $\partial_+ F(1) = +A(w)$ and $\partial_- F(1) = -A(w)$, proving Eq. (17). $\square$

Qualitatively, $\gamma > 1$ ($\Lambda_0 > k^*$) produces winner-take-all sharpening (amplifying the largest $|\alpha_j|^2$), whereas $0 < \gamma < 1$ ($\Lambda_0 < k^*$) flattens occupancies toward the uniform distribution $1/m$.

### Proposition 1. Exact closed-form finite-$\beta$ leakage at $\gamma = 1$

At the unique Born exponent $\gamma = 1$, the partition function $Z(\beta, 1)$ evaluates in closed form for every pointer configuration $\{\phi_j\}_{j=1}^m$ and every $\beta > 0$:

$$
Z(\beta, 1) = 2\pi I_0(\beta), \qquad \left|p_j(\beta, 1) - |\alpha_j|^2\right| \le \max\!\big(|\alpha_j|^2, 1 - |\alpha_j|^2\big)\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(\beta, \delta) \le \frac{(\pi - \delta)\,e^{\beta\cos\delta}}{\pi I_0(\beta)}, \tag{18}
$$

where $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(\beta, \delta) := \frac{1}{2\pi I_0(\beta)}\int_{|\theta| > \delta} e^{\beta\cos\theta}\,d\theta$ is the exact von Mises basin-tail leakage. In particular, for $m = 3$ equally spaced pointers ($\phi_j = 2\pi(j-1)/3$, $\delta = \pi/3$) with $q(\beta) := \varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(\beta, \pi/3)$, the finite-$\beta$ equilibrium occupancies satisfy the exact identity

$$
p_j(\beta, 1) = |\alpha_j|^2 - \frac{3}{2}\,q(\beta)\!\left(|\alpha_j|^2 - \frac{1}{3}\right) \qquad (j = 1, 2, 3;\ \text{all } \beta > 0). \tag{19}
$$

**Proof.** Integrating Eq. (1) over $S^1$ at $\gamma = 1$ gives $Z(\beta, 1) = \sum_{l=1}^m w_l \int_0^{2\pi} e^{\beta\cos(\phi-\phi_l)}\,d\phi = 2\pi I_0(\beta)\sum_{l=1}^m w_l = 2\pi I_0(\beta)$. Writing $p_j(\beta, 1) = w_j(1 - a_j) + \sum_{l \ne j} w_l b_{jl}$, where $a_j := (2\pi I_0(\beta))^{-1}\int_{S^1 \setminus B_j} e^{\beta\cos(\phi-\phi_j)}\,d\phi \in [0, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(\beta,\delta)]$ and $b_{jl} := (2\pi I_0(\beta))^{-1}\int_{B_j} e^{\beta\cos(\phi-\phi_l)}\,d\phi \in [0, \varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}(\beta,\delta)]$, yields $-w_j \varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}} \le p_j(\beta, 1) - w_j \le (1 - w_j)\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leak}}$, proving Eq. (18). When $m = 3$ pointers are equally spaced, symmetry gives $a_j = q(\beta)$ and $b_{jl} = \frac{1}{2}q(\beta)$ for every $j \ne l$, so $p_j(\beta, 1) = w_j(1 - q(\beta)) + \frac{1}{2}(1 - w_j)q(\beta) = w_j - \frac{3}{2}q(\beta)(w_j - \frac{1}{3})$, proving Eq. (19). $\square$

### Proposition 2. Deterministic ($D = 0$) gradient-capture obstruction

Consider the two-pointer antipodal landscape $C(\phi) = w_1 e^{\beta\cos\phi} + w_2 e^{-\beta\cos\phi}$ ($\phi_1 = 0$, $\phi_2 = \pi$, $w_1 + w_2 = 1$, $w_1, w_2 > 0$) under purely deterministic gradient ascent $\dot\phi = \frac{1}{\eta}\partial_\phi \Lambda(\phi) = \frac{\Lambda_0}{\eta}\partial_\phi \ln C(\phi)$ ($D = 0$) initialized from the uniform phase measure $d\phi/(2\pi)$ on $S^1$. Whenever $|\ln(w_1/w_2)| < 2\beta$, the unstable separatrix lies at $\cos\phi_* = -\frac{1}{2\beta}\ln(w_1/w_2)$ and the exact deterministic terminal capture fraction of pointer $1$ is

$$
P_{\det}(1; \beta) = \frac{1}{\pi}\arccos\!\left(-\frac{1}{2\beta}\ln\!\frac{w_1}{w_2}\right) = \frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2\pi\beta}\ln\!\left(\frac{w_1}{w_2}\right) + O(\beta^{-3}) \xrightarrow{\ \beta \to \infty\ } \frac{1}{2}. \tag{20}
$$

In particular, for every unequal state ($w_1 \ne 1/2$), deterministic ($D = 0$) gradient ascent on Eq. (1) from a uniform prior fails to recover the Born weight $w_1$ in the sharp-basin limit $\beta \to \infty$, whereas stochastic Fokker–Planck equilibrium ($D = k^*/\eta > 0$, $\gamma = 1$) converges exponentially to $w_1$.

**Proof.** Differentiating $C(\phi)$ gives $\partial_\phi \ln C(\phi) = -\frac{\beta\sin\phi}{C(\phi)}(w_1 e^{\beta\cos\phi} - w_2 e^{-\beta\cos\phi})$. Apart from the stable equilibria $\phi = 0, \pi$, the velocity changes sign precisely where $w_1 e^{\beta\cos\phi_*} = w_2 e^{-\beta\cos\phi_*}$, i.e., $\cos\phi_* = -\frac{1}{2\beta}\ln(w_1/w_2) \in (-1, 1)$ whenever $|\ln(w_1/w_2)| < 2\beta$. Every trajectory with $\phi(0) \in (-\phi_*, \phi_*)$ converges monotonically to $\phi_1 = 0$, and every trajectory with $\phi(0) \in (\phi_*, 2\pi - \phi_*)$ converges to $\phi_2 = \pi$. Under the uniform phase prior $d\phi/(2\pi)$, the measure of $(-\phi_*, \phi_*)$ is $2\phi_*/(2\pi) = \phi_*/\pi$, giving Eq. (20). $\square$

Proposition 2 resolves a central inconsistency of the 2025 draft: **nonzero stochastic diffusion $D = k^*/\eta > 0$ calibrated to $\Lambda_0 = k^*$ is mathematically indispensable** for the coherence-landscape equilibrium mechanism. Deterministic terminal capture depends on separatrix geometry rather than peak-height ratios; only noisy ergodic sampling converts $\Lambda$-depth differences $\Delta\Lambda = \Lambda_0 \ln(w_j/w_k)$ into Boltzmann–Born basin occupancies.

## 5. Reciprocity bookkeeping and carrier-level obstructions

A crucial advance of the 2026 FRC corpus is the separation of **canonical bookkeeping** (`FRC 566.001 v2.2`) from **candidate dynamical carriers** (`FRC 100.007 v3.3` and `FRC 830.005 v0.3`). The canonical entropy–coherence reciprocity law and its operational local ledger at declared register $\mu$ are

$$
dS + k^*\,d\ln C = 0, \qquad Q_{\mathrm{local}, \mu} := S_{\mathrm{local}, \mu} + k^*_\mu \ln C_{\mathrm{lock}, \mu}. \tag{21}
$$

As established in `FRC 566.001 v2.2` and `FRC 100.005 v2.3`, Eq. (21) is an exact operational bookkeeping hierarchy at a declared boundary and normalization, while its open-system physical universality remains a conjecture; the boundary probe of `FRC 100.005 v2.3` rejected a universal $k^*|\Delta\ln C|$ unlocking floor in one Langevin closure ($\sigma_{\mathrm{ST}} = 0.0288$, $\Delta\ln C = -0.132245$, threshold $k^*_{\mu_{\mathrm{nat}}} > 0.218$). Within this disciplined hierarchy, two independent obstructions narrow which physical carriers can host Born-preserving resonant relaxation.

### 5.1 Deterministic $\rho$-level $\Lambda$-drift obstruction (`FRC 100.007 v3.3`)

In `FRC 100.007 v3.3`, the original density-operator $\Lambda$-drift was audited and replaced by its trace-preserving anticommutator completion:

$$
\dot{\rho} = \mathcal{L}[\rho] + \frac{\alpha}{\Lambda_0}\left(\frac{1}{2}\{\nabla_\rho \Lambda, \rho\} - \operatorname{Tr}[\nabla_\rho \Lambda\,\rho]\,\rho\right). \tag{22}
$$

Although Eq. (22) preserves $\operatorname{Tr}(\rho) = 1$ (max trace error $6.88 \times 10^{-15}$) and positivity in the audited qubit trajectories (`|a0|^2 = 0.64`, pure dephasing rate $\gamma_{\mathrm{deph}} = 0.2$), both tested coherence functionals violate Born populations at the density-operator level:

- `C_super = 2|rho_01| + eps` drives $p_0$ downward toward $0.5$ (early drift rate $-0.00253/t$ to $-0.00874/t$, scaling as $\sim 0.09\alpha$ per model-time unit);
- `C_lock = (1 + |<sigma_z>|)/2` drives $p_0$ upward toward $1.0$ (early drift rate $+0.00652/t$ to $+0.02248/t$, scaling as $\sim 0.22\alpha$ per model-time unit); and
- an ensemble-member extension evaluated on two non-degenerate decompositions of the same Bloch-$z = 0.4$ state diverges in trace norm with small-$\alpha$ slope $\Delta / \alpha \approx 8.804$ at $t = 25$.

Consequently, deterministic $\rho$-level gradient climbs of operational coherence functionals are **excluded as Born-preserving carriers in the tested regime**. This motivates why `FRC 100.006 v2.0` works at the microstate-distribution level on $S^1$ rather than by adding a nonlinear drift directly to $\dot\rho$.

### 5.2 Qubit open-set affine/CPTP rigidity and $S^1$ fiber sharpness (`FRC 830.005 v0.3`)

A second, structural obstruction comes from `FRC 830.005 v0.3` (Zenodo DOI `10.5281/zenodo.24572231`; alphaXiv `https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.frc-830-005-quantum-born-reciprocity-obstruction`):

- **Proposition 4 (Open-set no-affine-channel rigidity):** On any nonempty open subset $E \subset \operatorname{int}(B^3)$ of the qubit Bloch ball with nonzero fixed-basis off-diagonal coherence $C_{\mathrm{off}}(\mathbf{r}) = \sqrt{r_x^2 + r_y^2} > 0$, **no affine map** $\Phi(\mathbf{r}) = T\mathbf{r} + \mathbf{t}$ of the Bloch ball can induce the normalized entropy–coherence ledger involution $D(s, y) = (-y, -s)$. This excludes every CPTP qubit channel (unital or nonunital), every positive trace-preserving map, and every fixed-ancilla Stinespring protocol on open coherent qubit subsets.
- **Remark 2 (1D-circle sharpness and open instrument gates):** The open-set hypothesis of Proposition 4 is mathematically sharp. Exact CPTP realizations exist on **one-dimensional circle fibers** $\mathcal{C}_{s_0, y_0} \cong S^1$ (both via constant replacement channels and via non-identity bijective $Z$-rotation unitary channels on the fixed-leaf circle $\mathcal{C}_{0.3, -0.3}$), and nonlinear **projective outcome-conditioned instrument maps** $\rho \mapsto M\rho M^\dagger / \operatorname{Tr}(M\rho M^\dagger)$ lie outside Proposition 4's affine hypothesis.

This structural result aligns tightly with `FRC 100.006 v2.0`: open-set affine density-matrix dynamics on $B^3$ cannot serve as a general reciprocity-lifting carrier, whereas **one-dimensional circular phase fibers $S^1$** and **outcome-conditioned stochastic/instrument realizations** remain structurally open. The alignment does not prove Half B; rather, it explains precisely why the $S^1$ microstate carrier of Sections 2–3 survives present-structure obstructions while full open-set Bloch-ball affine carriers do not.

## 6. Half B: honest statement of the open admissibility gates and dialogue with prior literature

Half A proves a clean conditional theorem: *if* a coupled system–apparatus interaction induces the Born-weighted landscape Eq. (1) on $S^1$ with $\Lambda_0 = k^*$ and $D = k^*/\eta > 0$, *then* any smooth initial microstate distribution converges exponentially to $\rho^*_1$, whose Voronoi basin occupancies match $|\alpha_j|^2$ up to the certified finite-$\beta$ overlap error of Eq. (18).

Half A does **not** constitute a self-contained, non-circular derivation of the Born rule. Four explicit admissibility gates define **Half B** and remain open:

1. **Gate B1 — Non-circular origin of $|\alpha_j|^2$ weights and $\Lambda_0 = k^*$.** In Eq. (1), the squared amplitudes $|\alpha_j|^2$ enter the coherence landscape by specification, and Corollary 1 proves that $\Lambda_0 = k^*$ is uniquely required to preserve their linear ratio. A non-circular derivation must show from an independently specified system–apparatus coupling Hamiltonian or symmetry principle why the effective microstate coherence functional weights basin $j$ linearly in $|\alpha_j|^2$ (rather than $|\alpha_j|^p$ for $p \ne 2$) and why the ratio $\Lambda_0 / k^*$ locks dynamically to $1$ rather than floating as an unfixed parameter.
2. **Gate B2 — Operational equivalence across mixed-state decompositions.** Any microstate-distribution realization representing a quantum state $\rho = \sum_k p_k |\psi_k\rangle\langle\psi_k| = \sum_l q_l |\chi_l\rangle\langle\chi_l|$ must produce identical observable outcome statistics for all operationally equivalent convex decompositions of the same density matrix $\rho$, and any bipartite extension $\mathcal{E}_{AB}$ on $\mathcal{H}_A \otimes \mathcal{H}_B$ must satisfy strict local marginals (no-signaling):
$$
\sum_k p_k\,P_{\mathrm{obs}}(\cdot \mid \psi_k) = \sum_l q_l\,P_{\mathrm{obs}}(\cdot \mid \chi_l), \qquad \operatorname{Tr}_B\!\big[\mathcal{E}_{AB}(\rho_{AB})\big] = \mathcal{E}_A\!\big(\operatorname{Tr}_B[\rho_{AB}]\big). \tag{23}
$$
   As demonstrated in `FRC 100.007 v3.3` §5, passing a symmetric mixture test at $\rho = I/2$ is a degenerate trap; Gate B2 requires non-degenerate same-$\rho$ controls (such as Bloch $z = 0.4$) and remote-steering audits.
3. **Gate B3 — Bipartite no-signaling (Gisin–Valentini constraint).** As shown by Gisin (1989, 1990) for nonlinear state-evolution laws and by Valentini (1991) for subquantum non-equilibrium ensembles, any departure from exact $|\psi|^2$ statistics in entangled bipartite states generically enables superluminal signaling unless equilibrium holds globally or the relaxation carrier is strictly linear at the reduced-state level. Any physical completion of `FRC 100.006` that permits transient non-equilibrium deviations (`FRC 100.006.002 v2.0`) must therefore state its bipartite coupling rule and either prove no-signaling or explicitly constrain the non-equilibrium regime to respect relativistic causality tests.
4. **Gate B4 — Explicit environment entropy accounting (`FRC 100.003 v3.3` Gate 3).** The KL H-theorem (Theorem 2) guarantees mathematical Lyapunov decay of $H(\rho \,\|\, \rho^*_\gamma)$, not automatic closure of a physical thermodynamic bath ledger. As recorded in `FRC 100.003 v3.3` §9 (where driving $\beta$ from $4$ to $12$ moved system-only $Q$ by $-0.37\text{ nats}$ while an uninstrumented accumulator captured only $0.089\text{ nats}$), Gate B4 requires an independently instrumented bath measuring standard entropy production $d_i S$ and heat/information export alongside locking.
