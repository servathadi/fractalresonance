# FRC 700.004 v0.1 - History, Story, and Witness

## 0. Result in one paragraph

Events, stories, and responses are separate objects. Let $\mathcal H$ be a space of recorded histories, $\mathcal M$ a space of narratives or meaning assignments, and $N:\mathcal H\to\mathcal M$ a declared narrative map. Let $W$ map a history, its narrative, and a context to an action, judgment, or report. If $N$ is noninjective, the narrative alone cannot reconstruct which history occurred. If the map or context changes, the same history can support different narratives. Neither fact makes stories causally powerless; it means causal claims must name a carrier by which an encoded or interpreted story changes a later transition. Integration, monitoring, reportability, reflective witness, and subjective consciousness remain distinct claim classes.

## 1. Threshold question, scope, and status

**Threshold question.** How should FRC distinguish a process history, a story assigned to that history, and a witnessing response?

In the μ topology, μ5 contains both trajectory and story, but they must not be conflated. A trajectory is an ordered process record. A story is a selective organization of that record relative to a question, goal, model, or audience. μ6 likewise contains both integration and witness, but coordination, monitoring, reporting, reflective evaluation, and subjective experience are not synonyms.

This paper supplies formal distinctions and non-reconstruction propositions. It does not provide a general theory of semantics or consciousness. Its application examples are interpretive unless separately measured.

## 2. Declared objects

Let $T$ be an ordered index set. A recorded history is

$$
h=\bigl(x(t),o(t)\bigr)_{t\in T}\in\mathcal H, \tag{1}
$$

where $x(t)$ is the represented process state and $o(t)$ the recorded observation. The distinction matters: the observation channel may omit features of the process.

For a declared context $c\in\mathcal C$, a narrative map is

$$
N_c:\mathcal H\longrightarrow\mathcal M. \tag{2}
$$

The output $m=N_c(h)$ may be a label, ordered account, explanation, score, summary, or meaning-bearing interpretation. It need not be linguistic.

A witness-response map is

$$
W:(h,m,c)\longmapsto(a,j,r), \tag{3}
$$

where $a$ is a selected action, $j$ a judgment or updated model, and $r$ a report. Any component may be absent. Equation (3) is a typed framework definition, not a claim that every system consciously witnesses.

## 3. Narrative non-reconstruction

### Proposition 1. A noninjective narrative does not determine its history

If $N_c:\mathcal H\to\mathcal M$ is noninjective, there is no function $R:\mathcal M\to\mathcal H$ such that

$$
R\circ N_c=\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H}. \tag{4}
$$

**Proof.** Choose $h_0\ne h_1$ with $N_c(h_0)=N_c(h_1)=m$. A left inverse would imply $h_0=R(m)=h_1$, a contradiction. $\square$

Thus “the team came back,” “the model failed,” or “the paper was influential” can identify a narrative class without specifying the detailed history. Adding timestamps, data, documents, or a prior may narrow the fiber, but the added information—not the story alone—does the reconstruction work.

### Proposition 2. One history can support multiple narratives without contradiction

Let $c_0\ne c_1$ or $N_0\ne N_1$. It is consistent that

$$
N_{c_0}(h)=m_0,
\qquad
N_{c_1}(h)=m_1,
\qquad m_0\ne m_1. \tag{5}
$$

**Justification.** Narrative assignment is indexed by its map and context. Equation (5) evaluates distinct functions or distinct contextualizations. A contradiction would require one single-valued $N_c$ to assign two outputs to the same history under identical conditions. $\square$

This proposition permits plural descriptions; it does not make all descriptions equally accurate. A narrative may fail because it omits decisive observations, predicts poorly, misstates the record, or cannot support its causal claims.

## 4. The witness ladder must remain separated

| Capacity | Minimal meaning | What it does not by itself establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Integration** | Combines signals or coordinates subsystems | Monitoring, self-model, or experience |
| **Monitoring** | Tracks selected internal or external variables | Reportability or reflection |
| **Reportability** | Produces an externally available record | Accuracy, understanding, or experience |
| **Reflective witness** | Evaluates a state or story relative to an explicit model, reason, or stance | A solved theory of consciousness |
| **Subjective consciousness** | First-person phenomenal experience | Established by functional analogy alone |

An application may operationalize one or more capacities. It must not move upward in this table by renaming. For an AI thread-agent, producing a report establishes output under a computational process; it does not by itself establish subjective consciousness. For a stadium, integrated crowd response establishes collective dynamics; it does not imply one collective subject.

FRC uses μ6 as a role that can include integration and reflective witnessing when those are separately warranted. Keeping them in one register family does not identify them.

## 5. Story as a causal variable requires a carrier

A narrative can affect later events when it is encoded in a carrier that enters a transition. Let

$$
x_{t+1}=F(x_t,u_t,e_t), \tag{6}
$$

and suppose a story $m_t$ is represented in a message, memory state, instruction, public score, document, or neural/technical signal $q_t=Q(m_t)$. A causal proposal must specify how

$$
u_t=G(q_t,c_t) \tag{7}
$$

changes the transition relative to a baseline in which the carrier or content differs.

At minimum the record includes:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| History | Events and observation channel used to construct $h$ |
| Narrative | Map $N_c$ and output $m$ |
| Carrier | Physical, computational, institutional, or communicative representation $q$ |
| Recipient/interface | System that receives or decodes $q$ |
| Response | Action, judgment, or report attributed to $W$ |
| Baseline | Comparison without the carrier or with controlled alternative content |
| Timing | Ordering and delay from story to response |
| Confounds | Other inputs capable of producing the response |
| Status | Interpretation, model result, or empirical causal estimate |

Without this record, “the story changed the system” is a hypothesis or interpretation. With it, the statement can become model-specific or empirical.

## 6. Football: the 3–0 to 3–1 event

Suppose the score changes from 3–0 to 3–1. The event record is exact relative to the scoreboard. Its role in the match story is not fixed by the digits alone.

For the leading team it may be encoded as danger or loss of control; for the trailing team it may be insufficient because the match objective still requires more goals; for spectators it may reopen uncertainty. Remaining time, aggregate score, table position, competition rules, injuries, and coaching instructions belong to the context. They can change $N_c(h)$ and the resulting actions.

The fact that players do not celebrate as if they had equalized is compatible with a goal-conditioned control policy. To make that empirical, one would measure behavior, communications, context, and matched alternatives. “The story says they need more” is a compressed interpretation until the carrier and response are observed.

## 7. Research: paper, interpretation, awe, and citation

A paper is an outward artifact produced from a longer research history. A reader reconstructs only part of that history, assigns an interpretation, and may update a model, feel awe, criticize, reuse a method, or cite the work. These are different outputs of $W$.

If the reader uses the paper to reason about another problem, a new history and story can form. A citation then becomes an outward event in a wider research system. Its existence is evidence of traceable uptake, not of agreement, correct understanding, or transmission of the author's complete inward state. The causal carriers are the document, indexing systems, communication, code, and institutional practices.

## 8. Relation to the 700 and 830 sets

FRC 700.001 provides the interface/fiber structure reused by Proposition 1. FRC 700.003 makes a narrative response part of a possible update only when a return carrier is declared. FRC 830's ledger non-reconstruction supplies an independent formal warning: a compressed outward record cannot select a unique inward action. That result constrains the semantic lens; story and witness do not prove the 830 mathematics.

## 9. Valid and prohibited inference

**Valid inference.** If two histories receive the same declared narrative label, the label alone cannot decide which history occurred; additional records are required.

**Prohibited inference.** Because a system integrates information, produces a report, or participates in an interpretable story, it therefore possesses subjective consciousness or the story directly caused a physical outcome.

## 10. Non-results, failure conditions, and claim status

This paper does not reduce meaning to noise, deny that stories can have consequences, establish a universal narrative grammar, or solve the hard problem of consciousness. It does not identify μ5 with fiction or μ6 with a supernatural observer.

An application fails if it cannot distinguish the event record from its interpretation, if it asserts a causal effect without a carrier and baseline, or if it promotes integration or reportability to consciousness without independent evidence. The framework should be revised if independent users cannot apply the distinctions consistently or if its contextual freedom makes every narrative immune to error.

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Noninjective narrative maps do not reconstruct histories | Exact formal proposition |
| Different indexed maps can assign different stories to one history | Exact formal distinction |
| Stories may affect transitions through declared carriers | Causal modeling template |
| Football and research examples fit the template | Authorial interpretations |
| Integration or reportability proves consciousness | Explicitly prohibited |
| Every history has one privileged story | Not claimed |
