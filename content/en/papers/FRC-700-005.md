# FRC 700.005 v0.1 - The μ Atlas

## 0. Result in one paragraph

The μ topology becomes methodologically useful only if people other than its author can apply it under fixed instructions and expose where it fails. This paper supplies an assignment record, a six-system authorial atlas, and a preregistered independent-rater protocol. A μ assignment is always indexed by object, role, boundary, and evidence status; absent and unresolved are valid outcomes. Version 0.1 reports **no independent-rater data**. Its atlas is an authorial test fixture, not a validation result. Future agreement would support usability, disagreement localized to particular roles would guide revision, and near-arbitrary post hoc assignment would defeat the framework.

## 1. Threshold question, scope, and status

**Threshold question.** Can independent users apply the FRC μ topology consistently across very different declared systems without treating analogy as evidence?

FRC 700.000–004 stabilized five distinctions: evidence versus interpretation, inward realization versus outward participation, whole versus component under boundary change, return versus recurrence, and history versus story or witness. The present paper tests whether those distinctions can discipline actual assignments.

This is a protocol and authorial atlas. It is a working draft with independent review pending. It does not claim that the cases share dynamics, that every register exists in every case, or that agreement would establish a physical μ ontology.

## 2. Assignment record

For case $i$, boundary $b$, and candidate object or process $o$, record

$$
A_{i,b}(o)=
(r,\;q,\;\ell,\;I_+,\;I_-,\;g,\;p,\;s), \tag{1}
$$

where:

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $r$ | Proposed μ role or **absent/unresolved** |
| $q$ | Assignment rationale tied to the role definition |
| $\ell$ | Selective interface or observation map |
| $I_+$ | Information retained across the threshold |
| $I_-$ | Information discarded or unresolved |
| $g$ | Testable link or operational check |
| $p$ | Prohibited inference |
| $s$ | Claim status: interpretive, formal, model-specific, or empirical |

Every record must also state the declared unit $U$, inside $X_b$, environment $E_b$, and boundary $\partial_b$. An assignment without a boundary is incomplete.

## 3. Fixed role instructions

Raters receive these definitions without the authorial answers:

| Code | Assignment instruction |
|---|---|
| μ0 | Prior ground; spacetime–vacuum ground for a physical realization, otherwise a declared inherited initial condition |
| μ1 | Physical constituents and dynamics available to the bounded unit |
| μ2 | Bounded cell or operational unit selected by the case boundary |
| μ3 | Relations, exchange, coordination, or regulation among units |
| μ4 | Organized control, logic, explicit model, or rule structure |

The semantic and boundary codes continue under the same assignment instruction.

| Code | Assignment instruction |
|---|---|
| μ5-T | Recorded trajectory or process history |
| μ5-S | Meaning-bearing story or narrative assignment |
| μ6-I | System-level integration or coordination |
| μ6-W | Reflective witness, evaluation, or explicit stance |
| μ7 | Declared boundary, outward participation, and represented environment |

The code **A** means absent under the declared case, and **U** means unresolved from the supplied information. The split codes for μ5 and μ6 preserve the distinctions proved necessary in FRC 700.004 while retaining the common μ register families.

## 6. Independent-rater protocol

### 6.1 Preregistration and materials

Before recruiting raters, freeze and timestamp:

1. at least 48 short items, with at least eight target items per case;
2. the boundary statement and available evidence for each item;
3. the role definitions in Section 3;
4. one practice set excluded from scoring;
5. primary and secondary metrics;
6. missing-data handling and exclusion rules;
7. acceptance, revision, and rejection criteria; and
8. the authorial atlas hash, kept concealed until scores are locked.

Items must ask for one primary category from $\{\mu0,\mu1,\mu2,\mu3,\mu4,\mu5\text{-T},\mu5\text{-S},\mu6\text{-I},\mu6\text{-W},\mu7,A,U\}$ plus a short rationale and confidence score. Boundary-ambiguous items may be included only if the ambiguity is itself declared and scored separately.

### 6.2 Raters and blinding

Use at least three independent raters who were not involved in writing the atlas. Raters receive identical fixed instructions and cases. They are blinded to the authorial answer, one another's labels, and aggregate results until initial scoring is locked. Familiarity with FRC is recorded but not used to reveal answers.

Adjudication occurs only after the raw labels and rationales are frozen. Adjudicated consensus must never replace the unadjudicated agreement statistics.

### 6.3 Metrics

Report exact category counts and raw agreement per item and overall. If item $i$ receives $n_{ij}$ ratings in category $j$ from $n$ raters, define

$$
P_i={1\over n(n-1)}\sum_j n_{ij}(n_{ij}-1),
\qquad
\bar P={1\over N}\sum_{i=1}^N P_i. \tag{2}
$$

Let

$$
p_j={1\over Nn}\sum_{i=1}^N n_{ij},
\qquad
\bar P_e=\sum_j p_j^2. \tag{3}
$$

Fleiss' multi-rater kappa is

$$
\kappa={\bar P-\bar P_e\over 1-\bar P_e}, \tag{4}
$$

when $\bar P_e\ne1$. Report confidence intervals by a preregistered item-level bootstrap or other justified method. Raw agreement is primary because $\kappa$ is sensitive to prevalence and category imbalance. Do not use qualitative labels such as “substantial” as a substitute for the preregistered decision rule.

Also report confusion matrices, especially μ5-T versus μ5-S, μ6-I versus μ6-W, μ7 versus environment, and A versus U. Analyze rationales before changing definitions.

### 6.4 Decision outcomes

| Outcome | Interpretation | Required action |
|---|---|---|
| High preregistered agreement across cases | The instructions may be methodologically usable | Replicate with new raters and cases; do not claim physical truth |
| Disagreement localized to roles or boundaries | Definitions or case statements are ambiguous | Revise those definitions/items and rerun on fresh raters |
| Agreement only after seeing authorial answers | Training conformity, not independent usability | Reject the run as validation |
| Broad near-chance or rationale-inconsistent assignment | Framework is arbitrary or underspecified | Revise or reject the atlas |
| Systematic domain-specific failure | Claimed cross-system portability is too broad | Narrow the scope |

The numerical cutoffs for these outcomes must be set before data collection. Version 0.1 does not choose them after observing results.

## 7. Current data statement

No independent rater has completed this protocol for v0.1. Therefore:

- no raw agreement value exists;
- no Fleiss $\kappa$ has been estimated;
- no confidence interval or confusion matrix exists;
- no claim of independent reproducibility is made; and
- the authorial atlas must not be described as consensus.

This absence is a research assignment, not a positive result.

## 8. Relationship to the series

FRC 700.000 supplies claim classes and the non-transfer rule. FRC 700.001 requires inward, outward, retained, and discarded information. FRC 700.002 makes roles boundary-indexed. FRC 700.003 requires return carriers and μ0$'$. FRC 700.004 separates trajectory, story, integration, witness, and consciousness. The atlas is the first joint usability test of those constraints.

FRC 830 continues to constrain any application that uses state/ledger maps. Agreement about a μ assignment would not prove a lift, self-duality, physical mechanism, or reciprocity law.

## 9. Valid and prohibited inference

**Valid inference.** If blinded raters apply frozen definitions consistently across fixed cases, that supports the methodological usability of those definitions under the tested conditions.

**Prohibited inference.** Inter-rater agreement—or the author's ability to fill every table—therefore proves that μ levels are universal physical layers or that the assigned systems share a common dynamics.

## 10. Non-results, failure conditions, and claim status

This paper does not validate the μ topology, report independent agreement, rank the systems, or infer consciousness for eggs, AI systems, teams, ships, or astronomical systems. It does not require complete assignments.

The atlas fails if assignments cannot be made without changing boundaries mid-item, if absent/unresolved categories are discouraged, if rationales do not track the definitions, or if post hoc adjudication is presented as initial agreement. The cross-system claim fails if performance is acceptable only in one familiar domain.

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Assignment record and fixed categories | Framework definitions |
| Six-system table | Authorial atlas, not independent data |
| Blinded protocol and $\kappa$ estimator | Preregisterable methodology |
| Independent inter-rater agreement | Not yet measured |
| Cross-system usability | Open research question |
