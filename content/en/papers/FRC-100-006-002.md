## 1. Premise and formal retirement of the 2025 heuristic window

In the conditional equilibrium framework of `FRC 100.006 v2.0`, Born-rule outcome frequencies $|\alpha_j|^2$ arise on the microstate phase circle $S^1$ as the sharp-basin ($\beta \to \infty$), unit-exponent ($\gamma = \Lambda_0/k^* = 1$), long-time ($T \to \infty$) stationary equilibrium of an overdamped Fokker–Planck dynamics. Whenever equilibrium is only approached rather than attained—because sampling occurs at finite horizon $T$, because the initial microstate ensemble is skewed away from equilibrium, or because the pointer-coupling strength $g(t)$ varies during measurement—outcome frequencies deviate from $|\alpha_j|^2$ with **mechanism-specific functional signatures**.

The 2025 first version of `FRC 100.006.002` (Zenodo DOI `10.5281/zenodo.17526499`) correctly identified those three qualitative mechanisms (`P-D1`, `P-D2`, `P-D3`), but inherited from 2025 `FRC 100.006` v1 an underived heuristic expectation of $|\delta P| \in [10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$ and relied on an informal drift sketch rather than the exact Fokker–Planck generator. Version `v2.0` **formally retires the $[10^{-4}, 10^{-3}]$ magnitude guess**: without an independently calibrated physical relaxation rate $\lambda_1$, diffusivity $D$, and coupling ramp rate $\dot{g}$, no universal numerical deviation amplitude can be predicted from first principles.

Instead, this paper replaces heuristic estimates with **exact, parameter-explicit diagnostic theorems** derived directly from the self-adjoint spectrum of the `FRC 100.006 v2.0` Fokker–Planck operator on $S^1$. The logic runs rigorously in both directions:

- if a genuine non-Born residual ever survives accepted decoherence and readout baselines (`FRC 787.787 v1.2` §12), protocols `P-D1`, `P-D2`, and `P-D3` separate finite-time truncation, preparation skew, and non-stationary ramps by distinct scaling laws; and
- whenever precision Born tests return **null results** at tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}$, the forward bounds supply sufficient accuracy conditions directly, while **conditional parameter exclusions** on $\lambda_1^{-1}$, $|\gamma - 1|$, ramp lag, and the audited `FRC 100.007 v3.3` $\rho$-drift pilot coupling $\alpha$ follow once mode-cancellation and cross-term cancellation are independently controlled (Section 6).

## 2. Self-adjoint Schrödinger reduction and spectral gap on $S^1$

Let $C(\phi; \alpha, \beta) = \sum_{j=1}^m |\alpha_j|^2 \exp(\beta\cos(\phi - \phi_j))$, $V(\phi) := \gamma\ln C(\phi; \alpha, \beta)$, and $\rho^*(\phi) = Z^{-1} e^{V(\phi)}$ be the smooth strictly positive stationary density established in `FRC 100.006 v2.0` Theorem 1. The Fokker–Planck generator $\mathcal{L}$ on $S^1$ takes the self-adjoint divergence form

$$
\partial_t \rho = \mathcal{L}\rho := D\,\partial_\phi\!\left(\partial_\phi \rho - \rho\,\partial_\phi V\right) = D\,\partial_\phi\!\left(\rho^*\,\partial_\phi\!\left(\frac{\rho}{\rho^*}\right)\right). \tag{1}
$$

### Proposition 1. Unitary Schrödinger reduction and strictly positive spectral gap

Define the unitary similarity map $U : L^2(S^1, (\rho^*)^{-1}\,d\phi) \to L^2(S^1, d\phi)$ by $\psi(\phi, t) := (\rho^*(\phi))^{-1/2}\rho(\phi, t)$. Under $U$, Eq. (1) transforms into the imaginary-time Schrödinger equation

$$
\partial_t \psi = -H_{\mathrm{FP}}\psi, \qquad H_{\mathrm{FP}} := D\,B^\dagger B = D\left(-\partial_\phi^2 + W(\phi)\right), \qquad W(\phi) := \frac{1}{4}(\partial_\phi V)^2 + \frac{1}{2}\partial_\phi^2 V, \tag{2}
$$

where $B := \partial_\phi - \frac{1}{2}\partial_\phi V$ and $B^\dagger = -\partial_\phi - \frac{1}{2}\partial_\phi V$ on $L^2(S^1, d\phi)$. Because $S^1$ is compact and $W \in C^\infty(S^1)$, $H_{\mathrm{FP}}$ is self-adjoint, positive semidefinite, and has purely discrete spectrum

$$
0 = \lambda_0 < \lambda_1 \le \lambda_2 \le \dots \to \infty, \qquad \lambda_1 \ge D\,\exp\!\big(-\operatorname{osc}(V)\big) > 0, \tag{3}
$$

with unique normalized ground state $\psi_0(\phi) = (\rho^*(\phi))^{1/2}$ ($\lambda_0 = 0$) and orthonormal eigenbasis $\{\psi_k\}_{k=0}^\infty$ in $L^2(S^1, d\phi)$.

**Proof.** Writing $\rho = (\rho^*)^{1/2}\psi$, direct differentiation yields $(\rho^*)^{1/2}\partial_\phi((\rho^*)^{-1/2}\psi) = (\partial_\phi - \frac{1}{2}V')\psi = B\psi$, and applying $(\rho^*)^{-1/2}\partial_\phi((\rho^*)^{1/2}\cdot)$ yields $-B^\dagger$. Expanding $B^\dagger B\psi = (-\partial_\phi - \frac{1}{2}V')(\partial_\phi - \frac{1}{2}V')\psi = -\psi'' + (\frac{1}{4}(V')^2 + \frac{1}{2}V'')\psi$ proves Eq. (2). Since $\langle \psi, H_{\mathrm{FP}}\psi\rangle = D\|B\psi\|_{L^2}^2 \ge 0$, every eigenvalue is nonnegative, and $H_{\mathrm{FP}}\psi = 0 \iff B\psi = 0 \iff \psi \propto (\rho^*)^{1/2}$, establishing simplicity of $\lambda_0 = 0$. Compactness of the resolvent of $-\partial_\phi^2 + W$ on $S^1$ gives discrete eigenvalues tending to $\infty$. Finally, the Holley–Stroock Poincaré bound on $S^1$ (where the uniform-measure Laplacian gap is $1$) yields $\lambda_1 \ge D\exp(-\operatorname{osc}(V))$, establishing Eq. (3). $\square$

## 3. Mechanism P-D1: exact finite-time truncation bound

### Theorem 1. Exact spectral-gap bound for finite-time relaxation (P-D1)

Let $\rho_0 \in L^2(S^1, (\rho^*)^{-1}\,d\phi)$ be any initial probability density, with Pearson $\chi^2$-divergence $\chi^2(\rho_0 \,\|\, \rho^*) := \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{(\rho_0(\phi) - \rho^*(\phi))^2}{\rho^*(\phi)}\,d\phi < \infty$. Expanding $\rho(\cdot, t)$ in the orthonormal eigenbasis of Proposition 1 gives

$$
\rho(\phi, t) = \rho^*(\phi) + \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k\,e^{-\lambda_k t}\,(\rho^*(\phi))^{1/2}\psi_k(\phi), \qquad \chi^2(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k^2\,e^{-2\lambda_k t} \le \chi^2(\rho_0 \,\|\, \rho^*)\,e^{-2\lambda_1 t}, \tag{4}
$$

where $c_k := \langle \psi_k, (\rho^*)^{-1/2}(\rho_0 - \rho^*)\rangle_{L^2}$. Consequently, at every sampling horizon $T \ge 0$, the total variation distance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(T) := \frac{1}{2}\|\rho(\cdot, T) - \rho^*\|_{L^1(S^1)}$ and the Voronoi basin-occupancy total variation $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}(T) := \frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^m |p_j(T) - p_j^*|$ obey

$$
\max_{1 \le j \le m} |p_j(T) - p_j^*| \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}(T) \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(T) \le C_0\,e^{-\lambda_1 T}, \qquad C_0 := \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\chi^2(\rho_0 \,\|\, \rho^*)}. \tag{5}
$$

**Proof.** Because $\int_0^{2\pi}(\rho_0 - \rho^*)\,d\phi = 0$, the transformed perturbation $(\rho^*)^{-1/2}(\rho_0 - \rho^*)$ is orthogonal to $\psi_0 = (\rho^*)^{1/2}$ in $L^2(S^1, d\phi)$, so its expansion starts at $k = 1$. Parseval's identity yields $\chi^2(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*) = \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k^2 e^{-2\lambda_k t} \le e^{-2\lambda_1 t}\sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k^2$, proving Eq. (4). By Cauchy–Schwarz,

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(T) = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} |\rho(\phi, T) - \rho^*(\phi)|\,d\phi = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{|\rho - \rho^*|}{(\rho^*)^{1/2}}(\rho^*)^{1/2}\,d\phi \le \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\chi^2(\rho(\cdot, T) \,\|\, \rho^*)}\sqrt{\int_0^{2\pi}\rho^*\,d\phi} \le C_0\,e^{-\lambda_1 T}.
$$

Finally, the data-processing inequality for the measurable partition $\{B_j\}_{j=1}^m$ gives $\max_j |p_j(T) - p_j^*| \le \frac{1}{2}\sum_j |p_j(T) - p_j^*| \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(T)$, proving Eq. (5). $\square$

**Protocol P-D1 (Finite-time horizon sweep and falsifier).** Vary the measurement/locking horizon across a predeclared schedule $T_1 < T_2 < \dots < T_K$ while holding preparation and landscape fixed:

- *Positive diagnostic:* Finite-time truncation must decay exponentially at rate $\ge \lambda_1$ with zero asymptotic plateau.
- *Falsifier:* A statistically significant time-independent plateau as $T$ increases falsifies finite-time truncation as the sole cause (pointing instead to static finite-$\beta$ overlap, $\gamma \ne 1$, or systematic calibration error).
- *Sufficient accuracy vs. conditional null exclusion:* Eq. (5) is a **forward upper bound**: a horizon obeying $\lambda_1 T \ge \ln(C_0/\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}})$ is *sufficient* to guarantee $\max_j |p_j(T) - p_j^*| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$. A single null measurement $|p_j(T_{\mathrm{exp}}) - p_j^*| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}$ does **not** automatically invert Eq. (5) into a necessary lower bound on $\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}}$, because higher eigenmodes ($k \ge 2$) can cancel the leading mode at one isolated horizon (for example, under $\mathcal{L} = \partial_\phi^2$ on $S^1$ with antipodal basins and smooth strictly positive $\rho_0(\phi) = [1 + a\cos\phi + b\cos(3\phi)]/(2\pi)$, setting $b = 3a e^{8 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}$ makes the signed basin residual $[a e^{-T_{\mathrm{exp}}} - (b/3)e^{-9 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}]/\pi$ vanish identically at $T_{\mathrm{exp}} = 0.1$ even though $a/\pi \gg \epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}} = 0.1$). To obtain a valid exclusion, let $A_1 := \big|c_1 \int_{B_j}(\rho^*)^{1/2}\psi_1\,d\phi\big| > 0$ be a calibrated leading-mode projection and suppose multi-time sampling or preparation calibration bounds the higher-mode remainder $R_{\ge 2}(T) := \big|\sum_{k \ge 2} c_k e^{-\lambda_k T}\int_{B_j}(\rho^*)^{1/2}\psi_k\,d\phi\big| \le \theta A_1 e^{-\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}$ for some $\theta \in [0, 1)$. Then the reverse triangle inequality gives $|p_j(T_{\mathrm{exp}}) - p_j^*| \ge (1 - \theta) A_1 e^{-\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}$, so a null bound $\epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}} < (1 - \theta) A_1$ yields the **conditional** relaxation-rate constraint

$$
\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}} \ge \ln\!\left(\frac{(1 - \theta) A_1}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}}\right) \qquad \Longleftrightarrow \qquad \tau_{\mathrm{relax}} := \lambda_1^{-1} \le \frac{T_{\mathrm{exp}}}{\ln\big((1 - \theta) A_1 / \epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}}\big)}, \tag{6}
$$

  valid strictly conditional on calibrated $A_1 > 0$ and controlled mode cancellation $R_{\ge 2}(T_{\mathrm{exp}}) \le \theta A_1 e^{-\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}$.

## 4. Mechanism P-D2: exact linearity in non-ergodic preparation skew

### Theorem 2. Exact linear-in-skew transient decay (P-D2)

Let $\nu \in L^2(S^1, (\rho^*)^{-1}\,d\phi)$ be any non-equilibrium microstate prior ($\nu \ne \rho^*$), and consider the skewed preparation family parameterized by $\delta \in [0, 1]$:

$$
\rho_0^{(\delta)}(\phi) := (1 - \delta)\,\rho^*(\phi) + \delta\,\nu(\phi). \tag{7}
$$

Because the Fokker–Planck semigroup $e^{t\mathcal{L}}$ is strictly linear in $\rho$ and leaves $\rho^*$ invariant ($e^{t\mathcal{L}}\rho^* = \rho^*$), the solution at every horizon $T \ge 0$ satisfies the exact linear identity

$$
\rho^{(\delta)}(\phi, T) - \rho^*(\phi) = \delta \cdot \Big(e^{T\mathcal{L}}(\nu - \rho^*)\Big)(\phi), \qquad \Delta p_j(\delta, T) := p_j^{(\delta)}(T) - p_j^* = \delta\,\kappa_j(T), \tag{8}
$$

where $\kappa_j(T) := \int_{B_j} e^{T\mathcal{L}}(\nu - \rho^*)\,d\phi$ is independent of $\delta$. In particular, the basin and total-variation errors satisfy the exact combined bound

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}^{(\delta)}(T) \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}^{(\delta)}(T) = \delta\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}^{(1)}(T) \le \frac{\delta}{2}\sqrt{\chi^2(\nu \,\|\, \rho^*)}\,e^{-\lambda_1 T}. \tag{9}
$$

**Proof.** By linearity of Eq. (1) and $\mathcal{L}\rho^* = 0$, $e^{T\mathcal{L}}\rho_0^{(\delta)} = (1 - \delta)e^{T\mathcal{L}}\rho^* + \delta e^{T\mathcal{L}}\nu = \rho^* + \delta e^{T\mathcal{L}}(\nu - \rho^*)$. Integrating over $B_j$ proves Eq. (8). Taking half the $L^1(S^1)$ norm gives $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}^{(\delta)}(T) = \delta\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}^{(1)}(T)$, and applying Theorem 1 to $\nu$ yields Eq. (9). $\square$

**Protocol P-D2 (Preparation randomization falsifier and subtle distinction).** At fixed horizon $T$ and fixed landscape, apply pre-equilibration or randomized mixing that shrinks the skew weight $\delta \to 0$:

- *Positive diagnostic:* Every basin deviation $\Delta p_j(\delta, T)$ must scale strictly linearly with $\delta$ and vanish as $\delta \to 0$.
- *Crisp falsifier:* Any **residual deviation surviving pre-equilibration randomization ($\delta \to 0$)** at fixed $T$ **strictly falsifies** preparation skew as its sole source.
- *Important subtlety:* Averaging an initial prior $\nu(\phi)$ over uniform phase shifts $\theta \in S^1$ maps $\nu$ to the uniform circle density $\bar\nu \equiv 1/(2\pi)$, *not* to $\rho^*$; therefore uniform phase scrambling still leaves a finite-time P-D1 transient bounded by $\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\chi^2(\text{unif}\,\|\,\rho^*)}\,e^{-\lambda_1 T}$ (Table 2). Erasing the transient completely at fixed $T$ requires thermal/pre-equilibration mixing ($\delta \to 0$) or extending $T \gg \lambda_1^{-1}$.

## 5. Mechanism P-D3: exact non-stationary coupling ramps and adiabatic tracking bound

Suppose the pointer-coupling control parameter $g(t)$ (for instance, the basin gain $g(t) = \beta(t)$ or landscape amplitude $A(t)$ as in `FRC 100.005 v2.3`) varies smoothly along a ramp schedule $g(t) = \tilde{g}(t/T_{\mathrm{ramp}})$ for $t \in [0, T_{\mathrm{ramp}}]$ over a compact interval $[g_{\min}, g_{\max}]$. Let $\rho^*_g(\phi) = Z(g)^{-1} \exp(V(\phi; g))$ denote the instantaneous equilibrium density with generator $\mathcal{L}_g$ and spectral gap $\lambda_1(g) \ge \lambda_{\min} > 0$. Define the **adiabatic tracking lag** $r(\phi, t) := \rho(\phi, t) - \rho^*_{g(t)}(\phi)$, which satisfies the exact inhomogeneous evolution equation

$$
\partial_t r(t) = \mathcal{L}_{g(t)} r(t) - \dot{g}(t)\,\partial_g \rho^*_{g(t)}. \tag{10}
$$

Two geometric invariants of the instantaneous equilibrium family govern the tracking error: the **Fisher information** $\mathcal{I}_F(g)$ and the **metric-drift supremum** $M(g)$,

$$
\mathcal{I}_F(g) := \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{\big(\partial_g \rho^*_g(\phi)\big)^2}{\rho^*_g(\phi)}\,d\phi = \operatorname{Var}_{\rho^*_g}(\partial_g V), \qquad M(g) := \sup_{\phi \in S^1}\big|\partial_g \ln \rho^*_g(\phi)\big| = \sup_{\phi \in S^1}\big|\partial_g V - \langle \partial_g V\rangle_{\rho^*_g}\big|. \tag{11}
$$

### Theorem 3. Non-asymptotic adiabatic tracking bound for non-stationary ramps (P-D3)

Let $y(t) := \sqrt{\chi^2(\rho(\cdot, t) \,\|\, \rho^*_{g(t)})} = \left(\int_0^{2\pi} r(\phi, t)^2 / \rho^*_{g(t)}(\phi)\,d\phi\right)^{1/2}$ denote the instantaneous weighted-$L^2$ lag. Along any smooth protocol $g(t)$ on $[0, T_{\mathrm{ramp}}]$, $y(t)$ satisfies the exact differential inequality

$$
\dot{y}(t) \le -\left(\lambda_1(g(t)) - \frac{1}{2}|\dot{g}(t)|\,M(g(t))\right)y(t) + |\dot{g}(t)|\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_F(g(t))}. \tag{12}
$$

Whenever the explicit **adiabatic ramp threshold** $|\dot{g}(t)|\,M(g(t)) \le \lambda_1(g(t))$ holds for all $t \in [0, T_{\mathrm{ramp}}]$ (so the effective damping rate is at least $\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min}$), the total variation and basin occupancy tracking errors satisfy

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}(t) \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(t) \le \frac{1}{2} y(0)\,e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min} t} + \frac{\sup_{s \in [0, t]}\!\big(|\dot{g}(s)|\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_F(g(s))}\big)}{\lambda_{\min}}\left(1 - e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min} t}\right). \tag{13}
$$

In particular, starting from initial equilibrium $y(0) = 0$ with a $C^2$ schedule $g(t) = \tilde{g}(t / T_{\mathrm{ramp}})$ ($\|\dot{g}\|_\infty = O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-1})$, $\|\ddot{g}\|_\infty = O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$), the tracking error satisfies the forward bound $O(\|\dot{g}\|_\infty / \lambda_{\min}) = O(1/(\lambda_{\min} T_{\mathrm{ramp}}))$ and the exact asymptotic initial-layer decomposition

$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}) \le \frac{\|\tilde{g}'\|_\infty\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{F,\max}}}{\lambda_{\min}\,T_{\mathrm{ramp}}}, \qquad r(t) = \dot{g}(t)\,z_{g(t)} - \dot{g}(0)\,e^{t\mathcal{L}_{g(0)}} z_{g(0)} + O\!\left(\frac{1}{T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^2}\right), \tag{14}
$$

where $z_g := \mathcal{L}_g^{-1}(\partial_g\rho^*_g)$ is the unique zero-mean solution of $\mathcal{L}_g z_g = \partial_g\rho^*_g$ (Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse on $\{\int f\,d\phi = 0\}$). Dropping the homogeneous initial-layer transient from Eq. (14) yields the pure-response error $r(t) = \dot{g}(t) z_{g(t)} + O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2}) + O\big(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-1} e^{-\lambda_1(g(0)) t}\big)$. Consequently, uniform $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ pure-response accuracy holds on $[0, T_{\mathrm{ramp}}]$ for well-prepared initial data $r(0) = \dot{g}(0) z_{g(0)}$ or zero initial speed $\dot{g}(0) = 0$, and after an equilibrium start $r(0) = 0$ with nonzero initial speed $\dot{g}(0) \ne 0$ once the **logarithmic transient cutoff** $\lambda_1(g(0))\,t \ge \ln(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}/\tau_0) + O(1)$ is exceeded (or conservatively $\lambda_{\min} t \ge \ln(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}/\tau_0) + O(1)$ for a fixed reference time unit $\tau_0 > 0$). In particular, at the endpoint $t = T_{\mathrm{ramp}}$ for any fixed $\lambda_{\min} > 0$, the homogeneous transient $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-1} e^{-\lambda_1(g(0)) T_{\mathrm{ramp}}})$ is super-algebraically smaller than $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ as $T_{\mathrm{ramp}} \to \infty$, whereas a bare $t \gg \lambda_{\min}^{-1}$ regime without logarithmic scaling (such as $t = \lambda_k^{-1}\ln\ln T_{\mathrm{ramp}}$) leaves an $O((T_{\mathrm{ramp}}\ln T_{\mathrm{ramp}})^{-1}) \gg O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ remainder. Exact $\Theta(1/T_{\mathrm{ramp}})$ endpoint scaling requires the nondegeneracy condition $\tilde{g}'(1) \ne 0$ and $\partial_g\rho^*_{g(1)} \ne 0$; smooth schedules with vanishing endpoint speed ($\tilde{g}'(1) = 0$) can exhibit faster $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ endpoint decay.

**Proof.** Differentiating $\frac{1}{2} y(t)^2 = \frac{1}{2}\int_0^{2\pi} r^2 (\rho^*_g)^{-1}\,d\phi$ carefully retains the time derivative of the weight $(\rho^*_{g(t)})^{-1}$:

$$
y\dot{y} = \int_0^{2\pi} \frac{r\,\partial_t r}{\rho^*_g}\,d\phi - \frac{1}{2}\dot{g}\int_0^{2\pi} \frac{r^2}{\rho^*_g}(\partial_g\ln\rho^*_g)\,d\phi.
$$

Substituting Eq. (10) gives three terms. First, because $\int_0^{2\pi} r\,d\phi = 0$, the unitary map of Proposition 1 gives $\int r(\mathcal{L}_g r)/\rho^*_g\,d\phi = -\langle \psi_r, H_{\mathrm{FP}}(g)\psi_r\rangle \le -\lambda_1(g)\,y^2$. Second, Cauchy–Schwarz in $L^2(S^1, (\rho^*_g)^{-1}d\phi)$ gives $|\dot{g}\int r(\partial_g\rho^*_g)/\rho^*_g\,d\phi| \le |\dot{g}|\,y\,\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_F(g)}$. Third, the metric-drift term is bounded by $\frac{1}{2}|\dot{g}| M(g)\,y^2$. Combining all three and dividing by $y > 0$ (using the upper-right Dini derivative at $y = 0$) proves Eq. (12). Under $|\dot{g}(t)| M(g(t)) \le \lambda_1(g(t))$, Eq. (12) becomes $\dot{y} \le -\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min} y + B$ with $B := \sup_s (|\dot{g}(s)|\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_F(g(s))})$; Gronwall's inequality gives $y(t) \le y(0)e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min} t} + (2B/\lambda_{\min})(1 - e^{-\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min} t})$. Applying $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{basin}}(t) \le \varepsilon_{\mathrm{TV}}(t) \le \frac{1}{2}y(t)$ proves Eq. (13) and the upper bound in Eq. (14). For the asymptotic expansion in Eq. (14), write $r(t) = \dot{g}(t) z_{g(t)} + w(t)$ with $r(0) = 0$; substituting into Eq. (10) gives $\partial_t w = \mathcal{L}_{g(t)} w - \ddot{g}(t) z_{g(t)} - \dot{g}(t)^2 \partial_g z_{g(t)}$ with initial condition $w(0) = -\dot{g}(0) z_{g(0)}$. Since both forcing terms are $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ while the homogeneous evolution of $w(0)$ on the frozen initial generator differs from the time-dependent propagator by $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ over the $O(\lambda_{\min}^{-1})$ initial-layer timescale and decays exponentially thereafter, Eq. (14) follows. $\square$

## 6. Total error budget, sufficient vs. conditional exclusions, and link to `FRC 100.007 v3.3`

Combining `FRC 100.006 v2.0` (Theorems 1–3, Proposition 1) with Theorems 1–3 of this paper yields the forward triangle-inequality error budget for any finite-horizon deviation from exact Born probabilities $|\alpha_j|^2$:

$$
\left|p_j(T) - |\alpha_j|^2\right| \le \underbrace{\left|p_j(T) - p_j(\beta, \gamma)\right|}_{\text{Dynamic lag (P-D1, P-D2, P-D3)}} + \underbrace{\left|p_j(\beta, \gamma) - p_j^*(\gamma)\right|}_{\text{Static finite-}\beta\text{ overlap}} + \underbrace{\left|p_j^*(\gamma) - |\alpha_j|^2\right|}_{\text{Exponent mismatch }\gamma \ne 1}. \tag{15}
$$

**Crucial distinction between sufficient accuracy and necessary exclusions.** Eq. (15) is a *forward upper bound*: guaranteeing that each of the three right-hand terms is at most $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}/3$ is *sufficient* to ensure $|p_j(T) - |\alpha_j|^2| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$. Conversely, observing a single null bound $\max_j |p_j^{\mathrm{obs}} - |\alpha_j|^2| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}$ does **not** by itself bound the three terms separately, because static finite-$\beta$ overlap (which flattens occupancies toward $1/m$) can cancel an exponent mismatch $\gamma > 1$ (which sharpens occupancies toward the dominant basin). Indeed, on the two-pointer antipodal landscape with $w = (0.75, 0.25)$ and $\gamma = 2$, continuity from $p_1(0, 2) = 0.5$ to $p_1(\infty, 2) = 0.9$ guarantees a positive gain $\beta_* \approx 0.902170$ at which the exact stationary occupancy is $p_1(\beta_*, 2) = 0.75$ identically—producing zero Born residual despite $|\gamma - 1| = 1$.

Accordingly, converting a null precision-Born observation $\max_j |p_j^{\mathrm{obs}} - |\alpha_j|^2| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}$ into a valid **parameter exclusion** requires explicit calibration hypotheses that isolate the targeted mechanism:

1. **Conditional exponent-ratio constraint ($\gamma = \Lambda_0/k^*$):** Require (i) nonuniform weights $w$ so $S_{\mathrm{exp}}(w) := \max_j |w_j(\ln w_j + H_{\mathrm{Sh}}(w))| > 0$, (ii) independent upper bounds $\delta_{\mathrm{dyn}}$ on dynamic lag and $\delta_{\mathrm{ov}}$ on static finite-$\beta$ overlap (e.g., via `FRC 100.006 v2.0` Eq. (12)–(14) at calibrated $\beta$), and (iii) the local linear-response neighborhood of $\gamma = 1$. Then the reverse triangle inequality yields $|p_j^*(\gamma) - |\alpha_j|^2| \le \epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} := \epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}} + \delta_{\mathrm{dyn}} + \delta_{\mathrm{ov}}$, giving the conditional local bound
$$
|\gamma - 1| = \left|\frac{\Lambda_0}{k^*} - 1\right| \le \frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}}{S_{\mathrm{exp}}(w)} + O(\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}^2), \qquad \epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} := \epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}} + \delta_{\mathrm{dyn}} + \delta_{\mathrm{ov}}. \tag{16}
$$
   For the 3-basin benchmark $w = (0.66, 0.24, 0.10)$ ($S_{\mathrm{exp}}(w) = 0.284784$), an independently isolated effective tolerance $\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} = 10^{-3}$ enforces $|\Lambda_0/k^* - 1| \lesssim 3.51 \times 10^{-3}$ locally around $\gamma = 1$.
2. **Conditional finite-time relaxation constraint (P-D1):** From Eq. (6), once a calibrated leading-mode projection $A_1 > 0$ and multi-time remainder control $R_{\ge 2}(T_{\mathrm{exp}}) \le \theta A_1 e^{-\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}}}$ ($\theta \in [0, 1)$) rule out finite-time mode cancellation, a null bound $\epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}} < (1 - \theta)A_1$ conditionally constrains $\lambda_1 T_{\mathrm{exp}} \ge \ln((1 - \theta)A_1 / \epsilon_{\mathrm{obs}})$. Without cancellation control, Eq. (5) remains a forward sufficiency condition only.
3. **Conditional adiabatic ramp-lag constraint (P-D3):** Eq. (14) gives a *sufficient* ramp duration $T_{\mathrm{ramp}} \ge \|\tilde{g}'\|_\infty\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{F,\max}}/(\lambda_{\min}\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}})$ to achieve accuracy $\epsilon_{\mathrm{target}}$. A conditional constraint $|\dot{g}(T_{\mathrm{ramp}})| \lesssim \epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}} / c_{\mathrm{ad}}$ follows only under nondegenerate endpoint speed $\tilde{g}'(1) \ne 0$, endpoint logarithmic transient cutoff $\lambda_1(g(0)) T_{\mathrm{ramp}} \ge \ln(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}/\tau_0) + O(1)$, independently bounded $O(T_{\mathrm{ramp}}^{-2})$ remainder and static/exponent terms, and calibrated nonzero adiabatic basin susceptibility $c_{\mathrm{ad}} := \big|\int_{B_j} z_{g(T_{\mathrm{ramp}})}\,d\phi\big| > 0$ (in Table 4 at $\beta = 6$, $c_{\mathrm{ad}} \approx 6.4 \times 10^{-3}$ per unit $\dot\beta$).
4. **Scoped link to the audited `FRC 100.007 v3.3` $\rho$-drift pilot:** In `FRC 100.007 v3.3` §3 and §5–6, deterministic $\rho$-level $\Lambda$-drift was audited on a qubit model at pure dephasing $\gamma_{\mathrm{deph}} = 0.2$: (a) early population-drift rates fell in $[0.09\alpha, 0.22\alpha]$ per model-time unit for `C_super` and `C_lock` across $\alpha \in [0.02, 0.10]$, and (b) non-degenerate same-$\rho$ preparation-dependence trace-norm divergence at Bloch $z = 0.4$, $t = 25$ had small-$\alpha$ slope $\Delta / \alpha \approx 8.804$ (saturating to least-squares slope $2.981$ over the full range $\alpha \le 0.10$).
