# FRC 700.000 v0.1 - The Threshold Charter

## 0. Result in one paragraph

The FRC 700.00x series is a series of **threshold papers**, not a new branch of physics. Its subject is the disciplined passage from one description to another. This paper establishes four claim classes, a forward evidence rule, a non-transfer rule, and a minimum threshold record. The FRC μ topology may organize an account of ground, constituents, units, relations, logic, history, story, integration, witness, boundary, and environment. That organization is an FRC framework convention. A proof, model calculation, or measurement may restrict a μ interpretation, but resemblance to the μ pattern supplies no proof and no physical evidence. This asymmetry is the charter's principal result.

## 1. Threshold question, scope, and status

**Threshold question.** When may a result in one descriptive domain legitimately change a claim in another?

A threshold is any declared passage among an inward realization, a selective interface, and an outward description or participation. It may retain, discard, translate, delay, or transform information. The scientific problem is not that such passages exist; it is that an argument can cross one without recording what changed.

This paper supplies a governance rule for later 700.00x papers. It does not establish that nature is divided into μ levels, that every system instantiates every register, or that a formal similarity is a causal mechanism. Its status is **framework, working draft, independent review pending**.

## 2. Four claim classes

Every material statement in the series must be identifiable as one of four classes.

| Claim class | Required support | What it may establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Interpretive frame** | Defined terms, coherent use, declared scope, and comparison with alternatives | A useful way to organize or ask about a system |
| **Formal result** | Explicit objects, assumptions, and valid derivation or proof | A consequence inside the declared formal system |
| **Model-specific result** | A formal model plus computation, simulation, or exact solution on a declared domain | Behaviour of that model and implementation |
| **Empirical result** | Operationalized variables, measurements, controls, uncertainty, and reproducible analysis | Evidence about the sampled physical or social system |

A statement can move between classes only by adding the missing support. Repetition, visual fit, breadth of application, or emotional force does not perform that promotion.

## 3. The evidence-direction rule

Let $E$ be an independently supported formal, model-specific, or empirical result, and let $I$ be an FRC μ interpretation. The permitted direction is

$$
E\longrightarrow \operatorname{restrict}(I). \tag{1}
$$

For example, if a ledger map is noninjective, an interpretation of the outward ledger may not claim unique reconstruction of the inward state. The result narrows what the interpretation may say.

The reverse direction is prohibited:

$$
I\not\Longrightarrow E. \tag{2}
$$

Finding a unit–relation–whole pattern in an example does not prove a categorical equivalence, physical law, causal coupling, or universal hierarchy. This is the **non-transfer rule**: evidential status does not cross a threshold merely because vocabulary or shape crosses it.

### 3.1 Valid inference

> FRC 830 proves that a particular ledger action does not determine a unique state action; therefore a μ description using that ledger must preserve the unresolved inward fiber.

### 3.2 Prohibited inference

> A system can be narrated in μ registers; therefore its physical dynamics obey FRC reciprocity or possess the state-space structure studied in FRC 830.

## 4. The FRC μ topology

The working topology is a toroidal nested-fractal conceptual organization:

| Register | Working role |
|---|---|
| **μ0** | Prior ground; spacetime–vacuum ground in a physical realization, otherwise the declared prior condition |
| **μ1** | Physical constituents and dynamics available to bounded units |
| **μ2** | Bounded cells or operational units |
| **μ3** | Relations, exchange, coordination, and regulation among units |
| **μ4** | Organized logic, control, explicit model, or formal structure |
| **μ5** | Process trajectory and meaning-bearing story, kept distinguishable |
| **μ6** | System integration and reflective witness, kept distinguishable |
| **μ7** | Declared system boundary, environment, and outward participation |

These roles are neither a ladder nor fixed properties of objects. A human may be treated as a μ2 unit at a team boundary while containing units and relations at an inward boundary. The assignment is indexed to the declared system.

The adjectives have restricted meanings:

- **nested**: a unit has inward composition and outward participation;
- **fractal**: the whole–unit–relation pattern may recur when the boundary is changed, with no claim of geometric self-similarity or fractal dimension;
- **toroidal**: outward participation may return through declared channels and update a later ground, with no claim of a physical torus or universal periodic orbit.

In a nonphysical example, μ0 may be inherited from the declared setup or left uninstantiated. It must not be filled by analogy merely to complete a table.

## 5. Minimum threshold record

Every cross-boundary conclusion in this series must declare:

| Field | Required content |
|---|---|
| Inward realization | State, process, or history on the declared inside |
| Boundary | What is inside, outside, and unresolved |
| Interface map | The rule connecting inward and outward descriptions |
| Retained information | What the outward description still distinguishes |
| Discarded information | Fibers, variables, timing, or uncertainty no longer distinguished |
| Transformation | Normalization, coarse-graining, encoding, selection, or interpretation |
| Return path | Any feedback channel, delay, memory, or environmental input |
| Claim class | Interpretive, formal, model-specific, or empirical |
| Failure condition | What would reject or force revision of the assignment |

If the map or mechanism is unknown, the record must say **unknown**. An empty field is more accurate than an invented bridge.

## 6. Relationship to FRC 700.777 and FRC 830

FRC 700.777 is the published origin note for μ-register scope. This charter does not silently revise it. It narrows the role of the 700.00x set: these papers test thresholds and inference discipline. Any later revision of 700.777 requires separate governance.

FRC 830 supplies independent formal constraints. In particular, its typed registers distinguish state spaces from ledgers; its strong morphisms require a commuting state/ledger square; and its lift and non-reconstruction results prevent a ledger action from being read automatically as a physical state action. The relationship is

$$
\text{FRC 830 formal result}\longrightarrow
\text{constraint on a μ threshold account}. \tag{3}
$$

FRC 830 is not validated by the μ topology. Nor does a μ label modify the canonical FRC relation

$$
dS+k^*d\ln C=0, \tag{4}
$$

where $k^*$ is the scale-invariant starred Boltzmann bridge for the declared operational realization. A μ register declares scope; it does not set, fit, or evolve $k^*$.

## 7. Misuse cases

The following uses violate the charter:

1. **Completion pressure:** assigning all μ registers even when a system provides no warranted instance.
2. **Analogy promotion:** treating a visual or verbal resemblance as a proof or measurement.
3. **Ledger realism:** treating two outward coordinates as the complete inward state.
4. **Boundary drift:** changing the system boundary while preserving the same μ labels without reindexing roles.
5. **Witness inflation:** using monitoring, reportability, or integration as proof of subjective consciousness.
6. **Return inflation:** drawing a cycle and claiming invertibility, recurrence, or conservation.
7. **Causal omission:** moving from story to physical outcome without a declared carrier and test.

## 8. Non-results and failure conditions

This paper does **not** provide a new physical law, universal μ ontology, consciousness theory, categorical self-duality, proof of toroidal dynamics, or empirical validation of the register map. It does not reclassify the 830 series as threshold work.

The charter should be revised or rejected if independent readers cannot distinguish its claim classes, cannot produce materially similar threshold records for fixed examples, or repeatedly infer promotions that the rules purport to block. It also fails as a useful method if recording retained and discarded information adds no diagnostic value over an ordinary system-boundary statement.

## 10. Charter for the set

Later 700.00x manuscripts earn their number only if they declare a threshold question, objects and boundary, a result or exact distinction, non-results, one valid inference, one prohibited inference, inherited dependencies, failure conditions, and claim status. Examples must be marked interpretive unless measured. Unresolved registers and mechanisms remain unresolved.
