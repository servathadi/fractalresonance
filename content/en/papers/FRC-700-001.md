# FRC 700.001 v0.1 - The Two-Face Threshold

## 0. Result in one paragraph

A unit's inward and outward faces are roles on opposite sides of a declared threshold. They are not two substances, and they are not the entropy and coherence coordinates of an FRC ledger. Write the inward realization as $X$, its environment as $E$, its boundary as $\partial X$, its outward-description space as $L$, and its selective interface as $\ell:X\to L$. The exact result is elementary but decisive: if $\ell$ is noninjective, no map from $L$ can uniquely reconstruct all of $X$. The interface therefore carries a duty to name what it retains and what it discards. FRC 830 independently strengthens the test for a proposed action by requiring a state/ledger commuting square; its non-reconstruction results constrain this μ interpretation, not the other way around.

## 1. Threshold question, scope, and status

**Threshold question.** What does it mean to say that one bounded unit has an inward face and an outward face?

The phrase is useful only after a boundary is declared. Relative to that boundary:

- the **inward face** is how the unit is realized by states, parts, processes, memory, and constraints on the inside;
- the **outward face** is how the unit becomes available for relation, action, observation, or description outside;
- the **selective interface** is the active passage that retains, discards, translates, delays, or transforms information between them.

This is a threshold-framework paper. The theorem below is exact set-theoretic mathematics. Its use as a μ interpretation remains a framework application. No universal physical boundary or mechanism is claimed.

## 2. Declared objects and boundary

Let

$$
U=(X,\partial X,E) \tag{1}
$$

be a declared unit, where $X$ is its admissible inward state or realization space, $E$ is the represented environment, and $\partial X$ records the modeling boundary and allowed exchanges. Let $L$ be an outward-description space and

$$
\ell:X\longrightarrow L \tag{2}
$$

the interface map. A point $z\in L$ is not assumed to be the unit. It is the outward record retained by this interface. The fiber

$$
\ell^{-1}(z)=\{x\in X:\ell(x)=z\} \tag{3}
$$

contains the inward realizations that the outward record does not distinguish.

The environment matters even when it is not in equation (2). Inputs from $E$, return signals, and boundary conditions can change which map $\ell$ is appropriate. Suppressing them is a modeling decision, not evidence that they are absent.

## 3. Exact non-reconstruction result

### Theorem 1. A selective interface does not generally reconstruct its inward realization

If $\ell:X\to L$ is noninjective, there is no map $r:L\to X$ satisfying

$$
r\circ\ell=\operatorname{id}_X. \tag{4}
$$

**Proof.** Because $\ell$ is noninjective, there exist $x_0\ne x_1$ with $\ell(x_0)=\ell(x_1)=z$. If a left inverse $r$ existed, then

$$
x_0=r(\ell(x_0))=r(z)=r(\ell(x_1))=x_1,
$$

a contradiction. $\square$

The theorem does not say reconstruction is impossible after adding information. It says the outward record $z$ alone does not determine a unique inward realization. A prior, auxiliary measurement, memory trace, or chosen section may select one member of a fiber, but the selection then depends on that added structure.

### 3.1 Finite witness

Let $L$ be any nonempty set and

$$
X=L\times\{0,1\},\qquad \ell(z,b)=z. \tag{5}
$$

Each outward point has two inward realizations. The hidden bit may stand for any unresolved degree of freedom; it is not asserted to be physical. Both $(z,0)$ and $(z,1)$ have the same outward face under $\ell$, so no function of $z$ alone recovers $b$.

This example is intentionally minimal. FRC 830.004 supplies a natural non-artificial instance: the declared qubit ledger has continuous state fibers, and inequivalent set-theoretic state involutions can induce the same ledger action.

## 4. The interface record

An outward face should be reported with the following record.

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Inside | What states or processes belong to $X$? |
| Outside | What is represented in $E$? |
| Boundary | Which exchanges cross $\partial X$? |
| Outward space | What distinctions can $L$ express? |
| Interface | How is $\ell$ defined or measured? |
| Retained | Which inward distinctions survive in $L$? |
| Discarded | Which fibers or variables are collapsed? |
| Transformation | Is there normalization, coding, aggregation, or interpretation? |
| Return | Can an outward event affect a later inward state? With what delay? |
| Uncertainty | What noise, missingness, or model ambiguity remains? |
| Status | Definition, formal proof, model result, measurement, or interpretation? |

Calling an interface active does not imply agency or consciousness. A membrane, sensor, API, measurement channel, social rule, or observer model can each be selective in different senses. The mechanism must be stated for the case at hand.

## 5. The FRC 830 commuting-square constraint

Suppose an outward ledger has an action $D:L\to L'$ and a proposed inward action is $\widetilde D:X\to X'$. Let $\ell':X'\to L'$ be the target interface. A strong lift requires

$$
\ell'\circ\widetilde D=D\circ\ell. \tag{6}
$$

The square is

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
X & \xrightarrow{\widetilde D} & X'\\
\downarrow\ell & & \downarrow\ell'\\
L & \xrightarrow{D} & L'.
\end{array} \tag{7}
$$

Equation (6) tests compatibility; it does not make $\widetilde D$ unique, continuous, physical, or empirically realized. FRC 830.005 proves the logical point using inequivalent state involutions with the same ledger action. FRC 830.004 strengthens it on a qubit register and separately excludes several physical operation classes on declared domains while leaving a general nonunital coherence-generating CPTP lift open.

The direction of support is one-way:

$$
\text{830 formal constraints}
\longrightarrow
\text{limits on the two-face interpretation}. \tag{8}
$$

The inward/outward metaphor supplies no proof of (6), no qubit result, and no physical lift.

## 6. μ reading

At one declared boundary, a μ2 unit may be composed inwardly from μ1 constituents and participate outwardly through μ3 relations. Its organized μ4 behaviour, μ5 trajectory or story, μ6 integration or witness, and μ7 environment depend on further declared maps. They do not arrive automatically from the existence of $\ell$.

The two faces also reindex. A human's outward participation in a team can become an inward component when the team is treated as the unit. FRC 700.002 develops this boundary change. The present paper establishes only the threshold triad:

$$
\text{inward realization}\longrightarrow
\text{selective interface}\longrightarrow
\text{outward participation}. \tag{9}
$$

## 7. Examples with bounded claims

### 7.1 A cell

An inward realization may include molecular concentrations and membrane state; an outward description may include secreted signals and measured surface markers. A marker panel is not the complete cell state. This is an interpretive template until a biological study declares measurements and mechanisms.

### 7.2 An AI thread-agent

An inward realization may include the available context, hidden computation, and tool results; an outward face is the message and artifact exposed to the user or another process. The message does not reconstruct every internal computation. This is a systems description, not a claim about consciousness.

### 7.3 A football team

Player states and team coordination form part of the inward realization at the team boundary; score, formation, and visible play are selective outward records. Equal scores need not imply equal team states. The example illustrates fibers; it is not an empirical sports model.

## 8. Valid and prohibited inference

**Valid inference.** If a declared outward map is noninjective, any downstream claim of unique inward reconstruction must add and justify information beyond that outward record.

**Prohibited inference.** A diagram with inward and outward faces therefore proves a physical reciprocity, a consciousness boundary, or a unique causal hierarchy.

## 9. Non-results, failure conditions, and claim status

This paper does not show that every boundary has two physically measurable surfaces, that every outward face is a ledger, that selection implies intention, or that a return channel exists. It does not identify the two faces with entropy and coherence.

The framework should be revised if users cannot declare $X$, $L$, and $\ell$ without arbitrary reassignment; if “retained” and “discarded” cannot be operationalized in applied cases; or if the two-face vocabulary adds no discrimination beyond an ordinary input/output diagram. A proposed application fails if it claims reconstruction while exhibiting an unresolved fiber.
