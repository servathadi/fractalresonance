# FRC 700.002 v0.1 - The Recursive Boundary

## 0. Result in one paragraph

An object does not carry one context-free μ number. It receives a role relative to a declared boundary. Write a bounded system as $S_b=(X_b,E_b,\partial_b)$ and the role of an object $o$ as $\rho_b(o)$. Then $\rho_b(o)=\mu2$ at one boundary and $\rho_c(o)=\mu1$ or a component-role at a larger boundary is no contradiction: the predicates are indexed by different systems. This makes the FRC topology nested. It is called fractal only in a restricted structural sense—the component–unit–relation pattern can recur after reindexing. The paper claims neither geometric self-similarity nor a literal universal hierarchy.

## 1. Threshold question, scope, and status

**Threshold question.** How can the same object be a whole at one boundary and a cell or component at another without changing what the object is?

FRC uses “cell” operationally: a bounded unit that participates in a declared larger system. The term does not require biological life. A biological cell, a person in a team, a software agent in a workflow, and a team in a league can occupy the unit role under different boundaries. Their mechanisms remain different.

This paper formalizes role indexing. The formal proposition is exact given the definitions; its usefulness across systems is a methodological hypothesis awaiting the audit in FRC 700.005.

## 2. Boundary-indexed systems and roles

For each declared boundary index $b$, let

$$
S_b=(X_b,E_b,\partial_b), \tag{1}
$$

where $X_b$ is the represented inside, $E_b$ the represented environment, and $\partial_b$ the allowed interface between them. Let $O$ be a universe of candidate objects and

$$
\rho_b:O\rightharpoonup R \tag{2}
$$

be a partial role assignment, with

$$
\begin{aligned}
R=\{&\text{ground},\text{constituent},\text{unit},\text{relation},\text{logic},\\
&\text{trajectory},\text{story},\text{integration},\text{witness},\\
&\text{boundary},\text{environment}\}.
\end{aligned} \tag{3}
$$

The map is partial because some roles may be absent, unresolved, or inapplicable. A μ label abbreviates one or more of these declared roles; it does not replace the boundary index.

For example,

$$
\rho_b(o)=\text{unit} \tag{4}
$$

means that $o$ is treated as a bounded operational unit in $S_b$. It does not state that $o$ is indivisible, autonomous, alive, or always a unit.

## 3. Whole–unit reindexing

Let $b\preceq c$ mean that the system declared at $b$ is represented within the inside of the system declared at $c$. A boundary-change record

$$
J_{b\to c}=(i_{bc},q_{bc},\Gamma_{bc}) \tag{5}
$$

contains an inclusion or representation map $i_{bc}:X_b\to X_c$, a role-reassignment rule $q_{bc}$, and an interface record $\Gamma_{bc}$ stating what detail is retained or discarded.

### Proposition 1. Indexed-role consistency

Let $b\ne c$. The assignments

$$
\rho_b(o)=r_1,
\qquad
\rho_c(o)=r_2,
\qquad r_1\ne r_2, \tag{6}
$$

are logically consistent provided roles are defined as boundary-indexed relations.

**Proof.** A contradiction would require both $\rho_b(o)=r_1$ and $\rho_b(o)=r_2$ with $r_1\ne r_2$ under a single-valued assignment at the same index $b$. Equation (6) instead evaluates two functions, or one indexed family of functions, at different indices. The situation is no more contradictory than one person being a parent in one relation and a student in another. $\square$

The proposition is modest. It does not prove that any particular role assignment is informative. It only removes a false demand for context-free labels.

### 3.1 Structural recurrence

At a fixed boundary the minimal pattern is

$$
\text{components}\xrightarrow{\text{organization}}
\text{bounded unit}\xrightarrow{\text{interface}}
\text{relations}. \tag{7}
$$

After changing boundary, the former unit may enter the component set of a larger unit:

$$
\text{unit at }b
\xrightarrow{J_{b\to c}}
\text{component or participant at }c. \tag{8}
$$

When an analogous component–unit–relation pattern is declared again at $c$, FRC calls the topology **nested-fractal**. This means structural recurrence under reindexing. It does not assert an exact scale transformation, geometric similarity ratio, power law, Hausdorff dimension, or universality class.

## 4. What must survive a boundary change

Changing the boundary is an information-changing operation. A valid $J_{b\to c}$ record states:

| Field | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Old and new systems | $S_b$ and $S_c$ |
| Object identity | How $o$ is tracked across descriptions |
| Old and new roles | $\rho_b(o)$ and $\rho_c(o)$ |
| Retained detail | Variables or capacities represented at both boundaries |
| Discarded detail | Inward states hidden at the larger boundary |
| New relations | Interactions that exist only in the larger description |
| Aggregation rule | How lower-boundary activity contributes to the larger unit |
| Failure condition | Observation that makes the reassignment unhelpful or false |

The record prevents “zooming out” from being mistaken for a neutral change of words. A player represented only by position and possession at the match boundary has lost much of the inward description available at the organism boundary.

## 5. Three bounded examples

### 5.1 Cell and organism

At a cell boundary, molecular species and organelles can be inward constituents, the cell is the unit, and membrane-mediated exchanges form outward relations. At an organism boundary, that cell can be represented as a constituent or participant in tissue organization. The role change does not erase cell autonomy; it changes which distinctions the larger model retains.

This is consistent with biological accounts of operational closure and autopoiesis, but the present paper does not infer biological closure from the μ assignment.

### 5.2 Human and research network

At an organism boundary, a human is the bounded unit. At a research-project boundary, that person can be a participating unit among authors, reviewers, tools, documents, and institutions. At a field boundary, the project itself can become one contribution among others. A paper's citation is an outward event in that wider system; it is not evidence that every inward intention has been transmitted.

### 5.3 Player, team, match, and league

A player is a unit at one analysis boundary and a participant in team dynamics at another. The team can then be treated as a unit within a match, and the match as an event within a competition. League tables and rules can shape match incentives while remaining outside the stadium boundary. Boundary indexing makes those changes explicit instead of forcing one permanent μ label onto “team” or “player.”

These examples illustrate application records. They are not measurements and do not establish that the same causal equations operate across the cases.

## 6. Relation to systems precedents and FRC 830

Near-decomposability and hierarchical organization have a long systems-science history. Simon's architecture of complexity, biological organization and autopoiesis, and Pattee's work on epistemic cuts and symbol–matter relations each motivate careful treatment of levels and interfaces. FRC's contribution here is narrower: boundary-indexed μ roles plus explicit retained/discarded-information records.

FRC 830 improves the account at any boundary where a state space is mapped to an outward ledger. Its non-reconstruction results require hidden fibers to remain visible in $\Gamma_{bc}$. The μ recurrence does not prove a morphism, lift, or duality in 830.

## 7. Valid and prohibited inference

**Valid inference.** If an object is reassigned from unit to component after a declared boundary change, conclusions about its larger-system role must be based on the variables and relations retained by that change.

**Prohibited inference.** Because a component–unit–relation pattern can be drawn at several boundaries, the systems share identical dynamics, a geometric fractal law, or a universal physical μ hierarchy.

## 8. Non-results, failure conditions, and claim status

This paper does not establish privileged natural scales, a hierarchy of value, biological life in technical units, or causal equivalence among cells, people, teams, software, and astronomical bodies. It does not require every μ role to be populated.

The framework fails an application if role assignments change without a declared boundary change, if incompatible assignments are made at one index without resolving ambiguity, or if the boundary-change record cannot say what information was lost. The broader method should be revised if independent users cannot reproduce the role changes on fixed cases or if nearly any reassignment can be defended after the fact.

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Different roles at different boundary indices are consistent | Exact definitional proposition |
| Component–unit–relation pattern may recur after reindexing | Framework pattern |
| “Fractal” denotes structural recurrence here | Restricted FRC definition |
| Examples instantiate useful assignments | Authorial interpretations, not measurements |
| The recurrence is geometrically self-similar | Not claimed |
| All natural systems obey one μ hierarchy | Not claimed |
